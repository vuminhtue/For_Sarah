## Overall Screening Recommendation

**Manual review required**

This study meets most inclusion criteria (1A, 1B, 1C, 1E, 1F, 1G, 1H) with clear evidence. However, criterion 1D is ambiguous because vocabulary was scored in "either English or Spanish," suggesting the sample was not strictly monolingual. Additionally, the study's primary research question concerns bidirectional relations between maternal teaching strategies and children's effortful control — the temperament-language (vocabulary) correlation is a secondary/covariate finding rather than a focal hypothesis, which a reviewer may wish to weigh when deciding on relevance and depth of inclusion.

## 2A. Citation Verification

## 2B. Relevant Study Number

- Single study, single longitudinal sample assessed at three waves (T1=18mo, T2=30mo, T3=42mo). Relevant to this review because children's vocabulary (a language measure) was assessed at each wave and found to correlate significantly with mother-reported effortful control (a Rothbart-based temperament construct), even though the primary research questions concern maternal teaching strategies.

## 2C. Study Design and Data Source

- **Design:** Longitudinal, multimethod, multireporter panel study with structural equation modeling (cross-lagged panel models)
- **Assessment waves:** Three waves — 18 months (T1), 30 months (T2), 42 months (T3)
- **Larger study/parent dataset:** Not explicitly named as part of a separately-titled larger project in this article, though the sample recruitment (full-term infants at three hospitals) appears to be a dedicated longitudinal cohort for this research group.
- **Recruitment setting:** Full-term, healthy infants with adult parents recruited at birth at three hospitals in a southwestern U.S. city.

## 2D. Sample Size

- **Total enrolled:** 255 (T1), 229 (T2), 209 (T3); one family with only father-reported data was dropped from these counts.
- **Final analytic sample:** 179 families participated at all three assessments; 55 participated at T1 but not T3.
- **Sample size for specific analyses:** Varies by measure/wave — e.g., nonparental caregiver questionnaires: T1 n=176, T2 n=153, T3 n=151; caregiver EC/vocabulary correlations computed with varying Ns per Table 3 (e.g., mother-report EC × vocabulary correlations based on ~205–247 depending on wave and reporter).
- **Reasons for exclusions/attrition:** Mothers who attrited (did not continue to T3) were significantly younger at the child's birth (M=27.16 years) than mothers still participating at T3 (M=29.70 years), t(243)=2.93, p<.01. No other demographic or key variables at T1 were related to attrition.

## Overall Quality Rating

**Good**

This is a methodologically strong longitudinal study: it uses a multimethod, multireporter approach across three waves, validated Rothbart-based instruments with documented reliability, appropriate statistical control for prior construct levels and key covariates (age, sex, SES), and substantial (though not complete) blinding of behavioral coders. The main limitations are the lack of an explicit power analysis and loss to follow-up modestly exceeding 20%. Given the overall rigor of the design and analysis relative to the other reviewed studies, "Good" is warranted, tempered slightly by these two limitations.
