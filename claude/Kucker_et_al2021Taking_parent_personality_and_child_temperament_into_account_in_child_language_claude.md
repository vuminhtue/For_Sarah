## 3. Stage 2: Study and Data Extraction

### 2B. Relevant Study Number
Single study with two independently recruited age-cohort samples (16- to 30-month-olds; 31- to 42-month-olds), analyzed in parallel. Both cohorts are relevant because Aim 2 explicitly examines temperament–language correlations (replicating/expanding prior temperament-language work) in each cohort separately.

### 2C. Study Design and Data Source
- **Design:** Cross-sectional, parent-report survey (correlational); two independent age-group samples, each measured at a single time point.
- **Waves:** One assessment per participant (not longitudinal).
- **Data source:** Original data collection via Amazon's Mechanical Turk (MTurk); not an archived/registry dataset, though data are posted to OSF post hoc.
- **Parent dataset/citation:** N/A — first report of this dataset (OSF: https://osf.io/mw3c2/).
- **Recruitment setting/location:** Online (MTurk), U.S. residents only, data collected December 2015–April 2019 (p.544).

### 2D. Sample Size
- 16- to 30-month-old group: N = 460 parents (210 female children)
- 31- to 42-month-old group: N = 328 parents (162 female children)
- 369 additional participants recruited but excluded: genetic disorder (n = 4), failed validity checks (n = 364) (p.543)
- Regression analyses: N = 459 (16–30mo, Table 4) and N = 325 total vocab / 313–325 other variables (31–42mo, Table 6)

### Comparison Type
Continuous language ability compared with continuous temperament (and parent personality) scores via (a) bivariate Pearson correlations, (b) partial correlations controlling for parent personality, and (c) hierarchical linear regressions (temperament/personality entered after child age [and gender]) to assess incremental variance explained.

### Overall Quality Rating
**Fair.** The study has excellent measurement (validated instruments, reported reliabilities), an explicit a priori power analysis, and appropriate statistical control for confounders (age, gender, parent personality). However, its cross-sectional, single-informant, single-time-point design (items 3, 6, 7, 10, 12, 13 rated No/NA/CD) limits causal inference and the ability to establish temporal precedence between exposure and outcome, which is characteristic of many parent-report vocabulary/temperament studies but nonetheless caps the overall quality rating below "Good."

## 7. Missing or Unclear Information Requiring Manual Review

- **1D (Ethnicity):** Not separately reported (only Race).
- **2E Other SES measure:** NA — Hollingshead-type composite not used in this paper (household income and education reported categorically instead).
- **2G:** Many facet-level (subscale) descriptive statistics beyond the three composite dimensions were not separately tabulated here (present in Table 3/5 as correlations only, not means/SDs at facet level).
- **2H:** Negative Affect correlation *signs* were reconstructed from narrative text because table formatting in the extracted PDF text dropped negative signs in some renderings — recommend a manual check of the original PDF's Tables 3, 5, 7, and 9 to confirm exact signs for all facet-level rows not reproduced here.
- **NIH Item 3 (CD):** Eligible participant pool for MTurk recruitment could not be determined (rolling online recruitment, no fixed sampling frame reported).
- **NIH Item 12 (NA/uncertain applicability):** Reviewer should confirm this reasoning; some may argue "CD" is more appropriate than "NA" if the tool intends a stricter application even for single-informant designs.
