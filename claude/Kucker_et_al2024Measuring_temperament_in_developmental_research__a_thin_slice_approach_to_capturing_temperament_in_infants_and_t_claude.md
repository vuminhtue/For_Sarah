## 3. Stage 2: Study and Data Extraction

### 2B. Relevant Study Number
Single study ("Study 1" per the preregistration reference). Relevant because its "Criterion Validity" analysis (Table 4) directly reports correlations between caregiver-reported (Rothbart/ECBQ) temperament and vocabulary (MCDI), and additionally validates a new thin-slice temperament measure against the same vocabulary outcome.

### 2C. Study Design and Data Source
- **Design:** Cross-sectional secondary/archival analysis of previously recorded videos and previously collected caregiver-report/vocabulary data ("post hoc" temperament assessment).
- **Assessment waves:** Single time point per child (one lab visit; videos divided into 3 sequential "thin-slices" of the same session, not separate longitudinal waves).
- **Data source:** Archived videos and data "collected in the first author's lab from 2015–2020" — a pre-existing dataset, not originally designed to measure temperament.
- **Parent study/dataset citation:** Not a single named external cohort; drawn from the authors' own prior lab studies (video corpus). Preregistration and materials at OSF: https://osf.io/jex53/
- **Recruitment setting/location:** "three different geographic locations from the southern, Midwest, and central United States"; children completed in-lab word-learning/cognition tasks.

### 2D. Sample Size
- Total videos meeting inclusion criteria (≥13 min, consent for future use): N = 516 (n female = 255)
- Missing caregiver-reported temperament: n = 22 (dropped for those analyses)
- Missing vocabulary data: n = 3 (dropped for those analyses)
- Interrater reliability subsample: 195 videos (37.8%) double-coded
- Vocabulary subsamples: MCDI:WS completers n = 369 (children ≤30 months); MCDI:III completers n = 146 (children >30 months); 3 missing vocabulary overall.

### Overall Quality Rating
**Fair.** The study demonstrates strong measurement development, reliability testing, and a preregistered, well-justified sample size (items 1, 2, 5, 9, 11 all "Yes"), and a notable methodological strength in blinding thin-slice raters to other data sources (item 12). However, it is a cross-sectional, single-time-point, unadjusted correlational design (items 6, 7, 10, 14 rated No/NA), and the representativeness of the archived-video sample relative to the original recruitment pool cannot be determined (item 3, CD), which together preclude a "Good" rating.

## 7. Missing or Unclear Information Requiring Manual Review

- **1D (`?`):** "All children were primarily English speaking" — unclear if this meets a strict monolingual inclusion criterion; recommend checking Supplemental Materials for an explicit monolingual/bilingual exclusion statement.
- **2E Child age range discrepancy:** Abstract states "Rangeage = 17–47 months" while the Methods/Participants section states "16–47 months (M = 27.51 months, SD = 6.78 months)." Recommend manual reconciliation of the exact range.
- **2E Developmental/diagnostic status:** No explicit exclusion criteria for developmental/neurological disorders were located in the excerpted text beyond the general framing of "typically developing" — recommend checking full Methods/Supplemental Materials.
- **2G:** Caregiver-report ECBQ raw score descriptive statistics (M, SD) and MCDI raw score descriptive statistics (M, SD, range) were not located in the provided excerpt — only reliability coefficients (α) and correlations were reported. Recommend consulting the Supplemental Materials or full-text tables for these values (coded `NR` throughout Section 4 above).
- **NIH Item 3 (CD):** Recommend manual review of Supplemental Materials for information on the size of the full video archive from which the 516 eligible videos were drawn.
- **NIH Item 12 (mixed rating):** Recommend manual confirmation of blinding procedures for caregiver-report data collection relative to MCDI administration.
