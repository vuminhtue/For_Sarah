## Overall Screening Recommendation

**Include**

This study directly and centrally tests the association between Rothbart-based child temperament (CBQ) — specifically Effortful Control — and stuttering severity (treated here as the qualifying communication/language outcome) in a sample of children who stutter, with detailed regression results reported. The only ambiguity is criterion 1D, since the sample is entirely children who stutter with no typically-developing comparison group; however, participants were otherwise typically developing (normal language/speech testing, no neurological impairment), consistent with the review's allowance for "apart from language delay or language disorder" populations.

## 2B. Relevant Study Number

- Single study, single sample. Relevant because it is centrally designed to test whether child temperament (specifically Effortful Control) predicts stuttering severity, using hierarchical linear regression models with mother- and clinician-reported severity as outcomes.

## 2C. Study Design and Data Source

- **Design:** Cross-sectional correlational/regression study (single time point/visit)
- **Assessment waves:** Single assessment (the "initial visit" of a larger longitudinal study)
- **Larger study:** Yes — participants were part of a larger longitudinal study investigating stuttering subtypes, funded by NIH-NIDCD grant R01-DC05210 (PI Nicoline Ambrose); this is the same broader Illinois-based longitudinal stuttering project referenced in related Ambrose/Yairi publications.
- **Recruitment setting:** Multiple Midwest sites — University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, University of Iowa, University of Wisconsin, Northern Illinois University. Participants were paid.

## 2D. Sample Size

- **Total enrolled:** 69 CWS (21 girls, 48 boys) and their mothers
- **Final analytic sample:** 69 (all participants included in all analyses per reported Ns)
- **Sample size for specific analyses:** All models used n=69 (mothers' HLM for mother severity rating and clinician severity rating)
- **Subgroup sizes:** NR (no subgroup breakdown beyond the total sample; no persistent/recovered classification reported in this article)

## Overall Quality Rating

**Fair**

The study has a clearly stated objective, well-defined and consistently applied inclusion criteria, validated temperament and severity measurement instruments with documented reliability, and appropriate statistical modeling (hierarchical regression with model comparison) to test the central hypothesis. However, it is a cross-sectional single-timepoint design (limiting several longitudinal-design NIH items), lacks power analysis, does not adjust for demographic confounders, and lacks blinding of raters to stuttering status. These combined strengths and limitations support a "Fair" rating.
