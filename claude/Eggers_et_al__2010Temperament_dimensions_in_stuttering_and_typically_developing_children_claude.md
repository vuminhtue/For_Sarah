## Overall Screening Recommendation

**Manual review required**

This study clearly meets criteria 1A, 1B, 1E, 1F, and 1G. However, criteria 1C and 1D are ambiguous: the study does not include a standardized measure of language ability, relying instead on stuttering diagnosis/severity as the communication-related construct — a boundary case per the review's qualifying-construct rules. Additionally, the CWS group is not "typically developing" (though this is the disorder-of-interest group, similar to how language-delayed groups are permitted under 1D). A reviewer should determine whether stuttering-severity-as-outcome sufficiently satisfies the language-measure requirement for this review's purposes, and whether the stuttering group is compatible with 1D's population requirements.

# Stage 2: Study and Data Extraction

## 2A. Citation Verification

- **Title:** Temperament dimensions in stuttering and typically developing children
- **Authors:** Kurt Eggers, Luc F. De Nil, Bea R.H. Van den Bergh
- **Year:** 2010
- **Journal:** Journal of Fluency Disorders
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jfludis.2010.10.004

## 2B. Relevant Study Number

- Single study. Relevant to this review because it directly measures Rothbart-based temperament (CBQ-D) in children with a communication disorder (stuttering) versus typically developing controls, and tests correlations between temperament and stuttering severity (a communication-related outcome).

## 2C. Study Design and Data Source

- **Design:** Cross-sectional group-comparison (case-control) study
- **Assessment waves:** Single assessment point (questionnaire completed once by mothers)
- **Larger study:** Yes — participants were part of a larger study described in more detail in Eggers, De Nil, & Van den Bergh (2009), which established the CBQ-D factor structure.
- **Recruitment setting:** CWS recruited with the assistance of speech-language pathologists practicing in Dutch-speaking Belgium; TDC recruited through the school system.

## 2D. Sample Size

- **Total enrolled:** 116 children (58 CWS, 58 TDC)
- **Final analytic sample:** 116 (58 CWS, 58 TDC), age- and gender-matched (±2 months)
- **Sample size for factor analysis:** Larger subject sample from the 2009 companion study used for factor analysis (146 TDC, 69 CWS) to reduce sampling error, separate from the 58/58 matched sample used for group-difference ANOVAs.
- **Subgroup sizes:** By stuttering severity: 8 mild, 35 moderate, 14 severe, 1 very severe (CWS group, n=58)
- **Reasons for exclusions:** NR (matched sample selection described, but no explicit exclusions/attrition reported for this specific 58/58 sample)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|

| Instrument | Dutch version of the Children's Behavior Questionnaire (CBQ-D; Van den Bergh & Ackx, 2003) |
| Informant | Mothers (all questionnaires completed by mothers) |
| Number of items | 233 items across 18 subscales |

| Reliability | Cronbach's alpha across 18 scales averaged .74 (from companion 2009 study); Van den Bergh & Ackx (2003) reported average internal consistency of .71 across 18 scales for 8–9-year-olds (n=71) | Method: Temperament questionnaire, p. 359–360 |
| Assessment time point | Single assessment |

## Overall Quality Rating

**Fair**

The study has clearly defined research questions, a well-matched case-control sample, validated instruments (CBQ-D, SSI-3/TSS), and prior validation of the underlying factor structure across groups (from the companion 2009 paper). However, it lacks power analysis, blinding, adjustment for confounders, and repeated temperament assessment. As a cross-sectional case-control design without a clear temporal exposure-outcome sequence, several NIH longitudinal-design items are not applicable, which also limits how strong causal conclusions can be. These combined strengths and limitations support a "Fair" rating.
