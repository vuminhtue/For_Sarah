## 2A. Citation Verification

- **Title:** Volubility as a mediator in the associations between conversational language measures and child temperament
- **Authors:** Laura Segebart DeThorne, Kirby Deater-Deckard, Jamie Mahurin-Smith, Mary-Kelsey Coletto, Stephen A. Petrill
- **Year:** 2011
- **Journal:** International Journal of Language & Communication Disorders
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1460-6984.2011.00034.x

## 2B. Relevant Study Number

- Single study using data from the Western Reserve Reading Project (WRRP), a longitudinal twin study. This paper is relevant because it directly tests correlations between temperament and multiple conversational language measures — the central purpose of the paper.

## 2C. Study Design and Data Source

- **Design:** Correlational, cross-sectional analysis embedded within a longitudinal twin study
- **Assessment waves:** Data drawn from the second (HV2) and third (HV3) annual home visits of a larger 4-visit longitudinal project; language measures averaged across HV2 and HV3; temperament questionnaire administered between HV2 and HV3.
- **Larger study:** Yes — Western Reserve Reading Project (WRRP; Petrill et al., 2006), a longitudinal population-based twin study of reading, math, and associated cognitive abilities.
- **Recruitment setting:** Twins recruited primarily from Ohio, with majority of families in metropolitan Cleveland, Columbus, and Cincinnati.

## 2D. Sample Size

- **Total enrolled (this analysis):** 161 twins (first-born twin within each pair selected to achieve an independent, non-twin-methodology sample): 73 monozygotic (41% male), 85 dizygotic (41% male), 3 undetermined zygosity (33% male)
- **Final analytic sample:** 161 (varies by measure — see Table 2 for measure-specific Ns)
- **Sample size for specific analyses:** Varies by language measure due to differing minimum sample-length requirements (e.g., MLU/NDW/NTW/TNC require ≥100 utterances; TCICU/D/uncut measures derived from all available data). Averaged (AVE) measure Ns range from 145–161 depending on variable (Table 2).
- **Reasons for exclusions/missing data:** Some children did not produce a 100-utterance sample at one or both home visits; in such cases, the "averaged" variable used the value from the single completed visit to maximize power.

### Comparison Type

## Overall Quality Rating

**Fair**

The study has clearly stated objectives, well-defined population and measures, validated instruments, and multiple time points for language sampling. However, it lacks a power analysis, does not statistically adjust for potential confounders (SES, age) within a single model, and does not employ blinding. The cross-sectional/correlational nature of the temperament-language relationship (measured concurrently) limits causal inference. These strengths and limitations together support a "Fair" rating.
