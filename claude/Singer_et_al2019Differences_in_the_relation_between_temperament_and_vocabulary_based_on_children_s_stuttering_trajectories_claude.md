### Overall Screening Recommendation
**Include** (flag for manual review re: population composition). All criteria are met with high confidence. The sample is not "typically developing" in the sense of being entirely free of any communication disorder — 36 of 60 children have a stuttering diagnosis (persisting or recovered) — but per the query's own criteria (1C explicitly allows stuttering as a qualifying language/communication outcome, and 1D allows language delay/disorder as an exception to the "no major disorder" rule), the study qualifies. A human reviewer may wish to treat the non-stuttering subgroup (n=24) and stuttering subgroups (n=36) separately when synthesizing evidence, since the central finding of this paper is precisely a *difference* in the temperament–language association across these subgroups.

## 3. Stage 2: Study and Data Extraction

### 2B. Relevant Study Number
Single study analyzing three talker groups (persisting stutterers, recovered stutterers, non-stuttering controls) drawn from a larger longitudinal parent project. Relevant because it is the first report from this cohort to jointly analyze CBQ temperament and vocabulary (PPVT/EVT) longitudinally, explicitly testing whether the temperament–vocabulary relationship differs by talker group.

### 2C. Study Design and Data Source
- **Design:** Longitudinal, repeated-measures observational study using linear mixed models (LMM).
- **Waves/timing:** Visits every 7–10 months, spanning 24–32 months total (up to 4 visits per child).
- **Larger study/archived dataset:** Yes — "Data were collected as part of a large-scale, longitudinal investigation of linguistic and emotional contributions to childhood stuttering at Vanderbilt University (Clark et al., 2015; Jones, Buhr et al., 2014; Zengin-Bolatkale et al., 2015)." This is "the first publication to report longitudinal CBQ and vocabulary data from these participants" (retrospective research questions).
- **Recruitment setting/location:** Vanderbilt University (Nashville/Middle Tennessee), via a free monthly parent magazine, referrals from the Vanderbilt Bill Wilkerson Center and other health professionals, and self-referral.

### 2D. Sample Size
- Originally entered parent study: N = 206
- Excluded (various reasons, Fig. 1): 108 total — began study >4;11 (n=12), non-compliant at first visit (n=5), speech/language ability below 17th percentile (n=25), did not participate 24 months (n=66: moved n=5, could not be contacted n=24, parent withdrew n=37) — stated attrition rate of 32%
- Met study criteria: N = 98
- Diagnosis at Visit 1: 57 non-stuttering, 1 unclassifiable, 40 stuttering
  - Non-stuttering: 12 excluded (later elevated disfluency/SSI), 19 excluded for gender-matching, 2 excluded for age-matching → final non-stuttering N = 24
  - Stuttering: 3 unclassifiable, 1 excluded (missing temperament data) → final: 10 persisting, 26 recovered
- **Final analytic sample: N = 60** (10 persisting + 26 recovered + 24 non-stuttering)
- Reasons for exclusion: explicitly reported in Fig. 1 flow chart and text (age, compliance, language percentile cutoff, attrition/relocation/loss of contact/parent withdrawal, gender/age-matching for control selection, unclassifiable stuttering status, missing temperament data)

### Comparison Type
A hybrid design: (a) continuous temperament scores used to predict continuous vocabulary scores via linear mixed models, AND (b) a three-level categorical "Talker Group" (persisting stutterers vs. recovered stutterers vs. non-stuttering controls — i.e., disorder groups vs. TD group) used as a moderator of the Temperament–Vocabulary association (Temperament × Talker Group interaction), with Time (visit number) as an additional repeated factor.

### Overall Quality Rating
**Fair.** The study has clearly specified diagnostic criteria, well-validated and reliable exposure/outcome measures administered repeatedly over an adequate longitudinal timeframe, and appropriate statistical adjustment for demographic confounders (items 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14 rated Yes). However, participation rate was below 50%, no sample-size justification/power analysis was reported, loss to follow-up (32% attrition) exceeded 20%, and exposure was not clearly measured prior to outcome in a way that established temporal precedence (items 3, 5, 6, 13 rated No), and blinding could not be determined (item 12, CD). These limitations, particularly the small persisting-group sample (n=10) and above-threshold attrition, prevent a "Good" rating.

## 7. Missing or Unclear Information Requiring Manual Review

- **2E Parent education / Household income:** Not separately reported in the excerpted tables; only the composite Hollingshead SES score was available. Recommend checking supplementary materials if available.
- **2E Ethnicity:** Not reported separately from Race.
- **2H Standard errors/CIs:** Only one SE value was explicitly reported (for the Negative Emotionality main effect, SE=10.678); SEs/CIs for other coefficients (post-hoc contrasts, F-statistics) were not provided in the text and are coded `NR` throughout.
- **NIH Item 3 (No, but borderline):** Reviewer should confirm whether the 32% attrition group (not participating 24 months) should be treated separately from ineligibility exclusions (age, non-compliance, low language percentile) when computing a strict "participation rate of eligible persons," as this could change the rating.
- **NIH Item 5 (No):** Recommend a full-text check (beyond the provided excerpt) to confirm no power analysis appears in an appendix or supplementary file.
- **NIH Item 9 caveat:** The use of two different CBQ form lengths (full 237-item vs. short 70-item) across participants is a potential measurement-consistency concern; although cross-form correlations are reported as high, a manual reviewer may wish to weigh this more heavily.
- **NIH Item 12 (CD):** Recommend manual review of the original methods (or a cited companion paper, e.g., Jones, Buhr et al., 2014) for explicit statements about examiner blinding to temperament/parent-report data.
- **Population classification (Stage 1, 1D):** Flagged for manual review — although coded `1` here per the query's explicit allowance for stuttering as a qualifying "language or communication outcome," a reviewer conducting a strict "typically developing" synthesis may prefer to treat only the non-stuttering subgroup (n=24) as eligible for a TD-only meta-analytic pool, and treat the persisting/recovered subgroups (n=36) as a separate "disorder-group comparison" evidence stream.
