**Exclude.** The article meets the age, temperament, language, empirical, Rothbart, and core-dimension criteria, but it does **not** report a direct temperament–language statistical association (1H = 0). Monolingual status is also unclear.

# Stage 2: Study and data extraction

## 2A. Citation verification
See citation above.

## 2B. Relevant study number
Single multi-site longitudinal cohort. Children who began stuttering were followed and later classified as persistent or recovered; an age/gender-matched normally fluent control group was included. It is relevant because both Rothbart temperament and standardized language were measured, even though they were not directly associated statistically.

## 2C. Study design and data source
Longitudinal observational study across four Midwestern U.S. sites. Visit 1 occurred within 12 months of stuttering onset, with follow-ups approximately 6 months, 1 year, 18 months, 2 years, 3 years, and 4 years later. The present article reports selected visits/domains from this protocol.

## 2D. Sample size
81 preschool children who stutter were initially recruited; 58 were classified for the reported analysis (19 persistent, 39 recovered). Twenty-three were not classified, largely because they left before status could be determined. There were 40 normally fluent controls.

## 2F. Measures

**Temperament.** Children’s Behavior Questionnaire, Short Form Version I; parent report (both parents when possible), 94 items, 1–7 response scale plus not-applicable. The instrument yields Rothbart dimensions including Surgency/Extraversion, Negative Affectivity, and Effortful Control. The article emphasizes mothers’ Visit-1 ratings in the reported temperament group analyses.

**Language.** Test of Early Language Development (receptive and expressive), Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test–III (receptive vocabulary), Expressive Vocabulary Test (expressive vocabulary), and conversational mean length of utterance. Standardized scores were used for the norm-referenced tests.

**Comparison type.** Group comparison across persistent stuttering, recovered stuttering, and normally fluent controls; no direct continuous temperament-versus-language association was reported.

The article reports a significant group effect for Negative Affectivity, F(2,88)=5.61, p=.005, partial eta-squared=.11, and language group effects at selected visits, but these are parallel group comparisons rather than direct temperament-language associations.
