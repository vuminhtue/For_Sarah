# Stage 2: Study and data extraction
## 2A–2D. Citation, study, design, and sample
Longitudinal observational study at Vanderbilt University with visits every 7–10 months over 24–32 months (up to four time points). Ninety-eight monolingual children were initially eligible. Final trajectory groups were persisting n=10, recovered n=26, and non-stuttering n=24 (total n=60). The paper explains exclusions for missing complete temperament data, later fluency criteria/classification, and age matching.

## 2F. Measures
**Temperament:** CBQ at every visit. Full 237-item and shorter 70-item forms were used. Emotional Regulation = Effortful Control (47 items full/22 short); Negative Emotionality = Negative Reactivity (62 full/23 short). Full/short correlations were r=.872 for both; Surgency was excluded because full/short correspondence was only r=.417. Parent ratings used a 1–7 Likert scale.

**Language:** PPVT-4 receptive vocabulary and EVT-2 expressive vocabulary at every visit, analyzed as age-referenced standard scores. PPVT internal consistency alpha=.95; reported test-retest reliabilities were .92 and .84 for PPVT/EVT respectively.

**Comparison type:** repeated continuous temperament predicting vocabulary across stuttering-trajectory groups in linear mixed-effects models, with group interactions.

## Overall quality rating
**Fair.** Repeated measures, validated instruments, clear eligibility, and adjusted mixed models are strengths. Small trajectory subgroups, substantial reduction from eligible to final analytic sample, lack of temporal precedence within visits, and unreported blinding limit quality.
