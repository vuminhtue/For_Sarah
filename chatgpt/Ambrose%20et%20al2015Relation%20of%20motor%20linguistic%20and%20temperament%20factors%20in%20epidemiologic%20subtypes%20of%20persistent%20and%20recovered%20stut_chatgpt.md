## Overall screening recommendation

**Exclude.**

The study clearly includes eligible-aged children and measures both Rothbart-based temperament and qualifying language abilities. However, it does **not** report a direct statistical temperament-language association, which is the key criterion in 1H. Monolingual status is also not reported and therefore remains uncertain, but the exclusion recommendation is based on the definite `0` for 1H rather than on the uncertain 1D item.

## 2B. Relevant study number

- **Study:** Single multi-center longitudinal study; no separate study/experiment number is given.
- **Relevance:** The same cohort was assessed in both the language domain and the temperament domain. However, the article uses these domains to compare Persistent, Recovered, and Control groups and does not statistically test temperament-language associations.

## 2C. Study design and data source

- **Design:** Multi-center longitudinal observational study.
- **Assessment waves:** Seven visits were planned. Visit 1 occurred within 12 months of stuttering onset; Visit 2 at 6 months after Visit 1; Visit 3 at 1 year; Visit 4 at 18 months; Visit 5 at 2 years; Visit 6 at 3 years; Visit 7 at 4 years. The article primarily reports Visits 1, 3, and 5, with disfluency also reported at Visit 6.
- **Larger study/cohort:** The article describes the work as the authors' "most recent longitudinal research program" and a multi-center project, but no separate registry or archived-dataset name is given.
- **Parent study/dataset citation:** NR as a distinct named dataset.
- **Recruitment setting/location:** University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, University of Iowa, Northern Illinois University, and University of Wisconsin at Milwaukee, with recruitment from surrounding Midwest communities. Recruitment sources included daycare centers, speech-language clinicians, health professionals, advertisements, and self-referrals.

## 2D. Sample size

- **Total enrolled CWS:** 81 preschool-age children who stutter.
- **Classified CWS used in reported group analyses:** 58 total: 19 Persistent and 39 Recovered.
- **Unclassified CWS:** 23, because they left before persistence or recovery could be documented.
- **Control group:** 40 normally fluent control children.
- **Main three-group analytic pool:** 98 children when analyses used all classified CWS plus controls, although sample size varied by domain/visit because of missing or unavailable measurements.
- **Motor subsamples:**  
  - Speech kinematics: Visit 1 P=1, R=4, C=6; Visit 3 P=2, R=7, C=11; Visit 5 P=7, R=14, C=10.  
  - F0 variability: Visit 1 P=9, R=22, C=18; Visit 3 P=14, R=34, C=24; Visit 5 P=7, R=17, C=9.
- **Temperament sample shown in Table 4:** Persistent n=19, Recovered n=35, Control n=37.
- **Reason for missing kinematic data:** Some children were uncomfortable wearing the helmet/transducer, producing smaller and inconsistent kinematic samples across visits.

### Reported inferential results relevant to the separate domains

Although not temperament-language associations, the article reports the following domain-specific findings:

- **Language:** Persistent children generally had lower standardized language scores than Recovered and/or Control children. Significant PPVT, EVT, and TELD-R group differences occurred at some visits; TELD-E and MLU/MLU-Z did not show significant group differences.
- **Temperament:** No significant group differences were found for Extraversion or Effortful Control. Negative Affectivity differed significantly among groups, with mothers rating Persistent children higher than Recovered or Control children. Within Negative Affectivity, Persistent children were rated as more fearful and less soothable.

## Overall quality rating

**Fair.**

The study has a clearly stated objective, well-described recruitment and eligibility procedures, repeated longitudinal measurement, standardized language instruments, and a validated Rothbart-based temperament instrument. Important limitations for the present review are the absence of a direct temperament-language analysis, no reported sample-size/power justification, no reported assessor blinding, attrition above 20% among the original CWS cohort, and no confounder-adjusted temperament-language model. These limitations prevent a `Good` rating, but the methods are sufficiently clear and systematic that `Poor` would be too severe.
