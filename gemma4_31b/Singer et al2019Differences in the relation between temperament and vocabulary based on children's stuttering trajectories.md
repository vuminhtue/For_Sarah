## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
- **Study Number:** Single study.
- **Analytic Samples:** The study focuses on three groups based on stuttering trajectories: Persisting (n=10), Recovered (n=26), and Non-stuttering (n=24).

## 2.3 Study design
- **Design:** Longitudinal study.
- **Assessment Waves:** Four visits over a period of 24–32 months (Section 2.2).
- **Data Source:** Part of a large-scale, longitudinal investigation of linguistic and emotional contributions to childhood stuttering at Vanderbilt University (Section 2).
- **Recruitment:** Advertisements in a parent magazine, referrals from Vanderbilt Bill Wilkerson Center, and self-referrals (Section 2.1).

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total Enrolled Sample:** 206 children in the larger cohort (Section 2.1).
- **Eligible Sample:** 98 children (Section 2.1).
- **Final Analytic Sample:** N = 60 (Non-stuttering n=24, Recovered n=26, Persisting n=10).
- **Exclusions:** 108 excluded from initial cohort (attrition 32%, matching, or missing data) (Section 2.1, Fig 1).

## 2.6 Population characteristics and eligibility
- **Eligibility:** Children aged 3;0 to 4;11; monolingual English speakers; no known hearing, neurological, developmental, academic, intellectual, or emotional problems, or language disorders other than stuttering (Section 2.1).

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Constructs:** Effortful Control (Emotional Regulation) and Negative Emotionality (Negative Reactivity).
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire (CBQ).
- **Informant:** Parent.
- **Number of items:** Full version (237 items) or short form (70 items). Effortful Control: 47 items (full) / 22 items (short). Negative Emotionality: 62 items (full) / 23 items (short) (Section 2.3.2).
- **Response scale:** 7-point Likert scale (1 = 'extremely untrue' to 7 = 'extremely true') (Section 2.3.2).
- **Reliability:** NR (though authors cite high correlations between full and short forms, $r = .872$) (Section 2.3.2).
- **Assessment Time Point:** Every visit (4 visits) (Section 2.2).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct 1:** Receptive Vocabulary.
    - **Instrument:** Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test, 4th edition (PPVT-4).
    - **Category:** Receptive.
    - **Score type:** Standard score.
    - **Reliability:** $\alpha = .95$, test-retest $\alpha = .92$ (Section 2.3.1).
    - **Assessment Time Point:** Every visit (4 visits).
- **Construct 2:** Expressive Vocabulary.
    - **Instrument:** Expressive Vocabulary Test, 2nd edition (EVT-2).
    - **Category:** Expressive.
    - **Score type:** Standard score.
    - **Reliability:** test-retest $\alpha = .84$ (Section 2.3.1).
    - **Assessment Time Point:** Every visit (4 visits).

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Stuttering Severity:** Stuttering Severity Instrument (SSI) (Section 2.1.1).
- **SES:** Hollingshead Four-Factor Index of Social Positions (Section 2.2).

## 3.4 Comparison Type
- Continuous language ability (PPVT/EVT scores) compared with continuous temperament scores (CBQ), moderated by stuttering group (Persisting vs. Recovered vs. Non-stuttering), using Linear Mixed-Effects Models (Section 2.4.2).

# 5. Temperament–Language Associations

**Substantive Conclusion:** Receptive vocabulary is negatively associated with negative emotionality specifically in children who stutter (both those who persist and those who recover), whereas this association is not present in children who do not stutter. No significant associations were found for expressive vocabulary or emotional regulation.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
- **Talker Group $\rightarrow$ Receptive Vocab:** Significant main effect ($p = .004$, $d = 0.380$), with non-stutterers scoring higher on average (Section 3.2.1).
- **Age $\rightarrow$ Expressive Vocab:** Significant main effect ($p = .012$, $d = 0.411$) (Section 3.2.2).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Longitudinal design with repeated measures.
- Use of standardized language tests and a validated temperament scale.
- Rigorous statistical control for age, gender, and SES using Linear Mixed-Effects Models.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Small sample size (especially the persisting group, $n=10$), which limits statistical power.
- High attrition rate ($32\%$).
- Lack of blinding for outcome assessors.
- Concurrent measurement of exposure and outcome (prevents causal inference).

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include.**

The study provides valuable longitudinal evidence that the relationship between negative emotionality and receptive vocabulary is differentially expressed in children who stutter compared to those who do not. Despite small sample sizes, the use of LMMs and standardized measures makes the data scientifically relevant.
