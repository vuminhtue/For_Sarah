## 2.6 Population characteristics and eligibility
- **CWS Eligibility:** Produced $\ge 3$ within-word disfluencies or monosyllabic word repetitions per 100 words of spontaneous speech and scored at least 'mild' on the Stuttering Severity Instrument-3 or the Dutch severity scale (Section 2.1).
- **TDC Eligibility:** Matched for age ($\pm 2$ months) and gender to CWS; no reported speech, language, hearing, neurological, or psychological problems (Section 2.1).

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-Dutch (CBQ-D).
- **Constructs:** 18 subscales clustering into 3 composite factors: Extraversion/Surgency, Negative Affectivity, and Effortful Control (Section 1.1, 2.2).
- **Informant:** Mothers (Section 2.3).
- **Response Scale:** 7-point Likert scale ('extremely untrue' to 'extremely true') (Section 2.2).
- **Reliability:** Average Cronbach's alpha = .74 (Section 2.2).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct:** Stuttering status and severity.
- **Instrument:** Stuttering Severity Instrument-3 (SSI-3) and/or Test for Stutter Severity-Readers/Non-Readers (Dutch scale) (Section 2.1).
- **Category:** Communication/fluency disorder.
- **Score Type:** Categorical (Mild, Moderate, Severe, Very Severe) (Section 2.1).

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Therapy Duration:** Measured in months for CWS (Section 2.1).

**Substantive Conclusion:** Children who stutter (CWS) exhibit significantly higher levels of Negative Affectivity (specifically Anger/Frustration) and Extraversion/Surgency (specifically Approach and Motor Activation), and significantly lower levels of Effortful Control (specifically Inhibitory Control and Attentional Shifting) compared to typically developing children. However, within the CWS group, these temperament traits were not significantly associated with the severity of the stuttering or the duration of therapy.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
The authors discuss the potential relationship between the found temperament deficits (Effortful Control) and working memory based on prior literature (Wolfe & Bell, 2004), suggesting a possible pathway via the anterior attentional network.

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Use of a validated, theory-driven temperament instrument (CBQ-D).
- Precise matching of age and gender between CWS and TDC groups.
- Clear reporting of both composite and individual scale results.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional design prevents causal inferences.
- Lack of control for potential confounding variables (e.g., socioeconomic status).
- Small sample size for a group comparison study without a power analysis.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include.** The study provides high-quality data on the relationship between a specific communication disorder (stuttering) and the Rothbart temperament framework, confirming differences in Effortful Control and Negative Affectivity.
