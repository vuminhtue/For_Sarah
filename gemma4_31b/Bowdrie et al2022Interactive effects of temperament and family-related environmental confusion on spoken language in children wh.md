# 2. Study and Data Extraction

## 2.1 Bibliographic information
- **Title:** Interactive Effects of Temperament and Family-Related Environmental Confusion on Spoken Language in Children Who Are Deaf and Hard of Hearing
- **Authors:** Kristina Bowdrie, Rachael Frush Holt, Derek M. Houston
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research
- **DOI:** 10.1044/2022_JSLHR-21-00665

## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
The manuscript describes a single study comparing two main groups:
- **Typical Hearing (TH) group:** Control sample.
- **Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) group:** Target sample.
This study is relevant as it examines the association between temperament (CBQ-SF) and spoken language (CELF), moderated by environmental confusion.

## 2.3 Study design
- **Design:** Cross-sectional observational study (p. 11).
- **Data Source:** Families were recruited as part of a larger longitudinal study on developmental outcomes in children who are DHH (p. 7).
- **Setting:** Home visits (p. 11).

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled:** Not explicitly reported.
- **Final analytic sample:** $N = 117$ (TH: $n=59$; DHH: $n=58$) (p. 7).
- **Subgroups (DHH):** HA users ($n=26$), CI users ($n=32$) (p. 7, Table 1).

## 2.6 Population characteristics and eligibility
- **Inclusion:** Children 3–7 years old; DHH must have mild-to-profound sensorineural hearing loss; TH must pass behavioral hearing screening ($\le 20$ dB HL); must score within 2 SDs of mean on nonverbal IQ test (DAS-II); spoken English at home; goal to learn spoken language (p. 8).
- **Exclusion:** Children older than 7 years (p. 7); reported neurodevelopmental difficulties apart from those related to hearing loss (p. 8).

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire - Short Form (CBQ-SF).
- **Informant:** Primary caregiver.
- **Facets Measured:** 
    - **Effortful Control:** (Attentional Focusing, Inhibitory Control, Low Intensity Pleasure, Perceptual Sensitivity).
    - **Surgency-Extraversion:** (Activity Level, High-Intensity Pleasure, Impulsivity, Reversed Shyness).
    - **Negative Affectivity:** (Anger/Frustration, Discomfort, Fear, Sadness, Reversed Falling Reactivity/Soothability).
- **Response Scale:** Likert scale (1 = extremely untrue to 7 = extremely true).
- **Reliability:** $\alpha = .62$ to $.88$ (p. 9).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Instrument:** CELF Preschool-2 (for children < 6y) or CELF-5 (for children $\ge 6$y).
- **Construct:** Receptive spoken language (Concepts and Following Directions subscale for Preschool-2; Following Directions subscale for CELF-5).
- **Category:** Receptive language.
- **Score Type:** Scaled scores.
- **Reliability:** NR.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Environmental Confusion:** Confusion, Hubbub, and Order Scale (CHAOS). Caregiver report, 15 items, Likert 1–4. Higher scores = higher confusion. $\alpha = .79$ (p. 10).

### Moderation Results (DHH Group Only)
**Interaction:** Effortful Control $\times$ Environmental Confusion $\rightarrow$ Receptive Language.
- **Effect:** Significant interaction ($\beta = -0.32, p = .001$, Table 4).
- **Conditional Effects (Simple Slopes):**
    - Low Environmental Confusion (16th percentile): $b = 4.90, p < .001$ (Positive)
    - Moderate Environmental Confusion (50th percentile): $b = 2.41, p < .001$ (Positive)
    - High Environmental Confusion (84th percentile): $b = -0.82, p = .385$ (Nonsignificant)
- **Johnson-Neyman threshold:** Moderation occurred at CHAOS scores of 29.15 or below ($b = 1.71, p = .010$).

**Substantive Conclusion:** For children who are DHH, higher effortful control is associated with better spoken language outcomes, but this benefit is only realized in homes with low-to-moderate levels of environmental confusion. In chaotic environments, the association disappears.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

- **Temperament $\leftrightarrow$ Environment:** In TH children, Effortful Control was negatively associated with CHAOS ($r = -.277$), while Negative Affectivity ($r = .337$) and Surgency ($r = .279$) were positively associated. In DHH children, Effortful Control was also negatively associated with CHAOS ($r = -.270$).
- **Inter-Temperament (DHH only):** Effortful control was negatively associated with negative affectivity ($r = -.282$) and surgency ($r = -.339$).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Use of standardized, widely accepted instruments for temperament (CBQ-SF) and language (CELF).
- Inclusion of a matched control group (TH).
- Statistical adjustment for socioeconomic confounding (caregiver education).
- Sophisticated moderation analysis using both pick-a-point and Johnson-Neyman methods.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inferences.
- Small sample size ($N=117$) may limit the power of interaction effects.
- Lack of blinding for assessors.
- Reliance on parent-report for temperament and environmental confusion.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include.** The study provides high-quality empirical data on the interaction between a core Rothbart dimension (Effortful Control) and language in a specific clinical population (DHH), adding nuance to the temperament-language relationship.
