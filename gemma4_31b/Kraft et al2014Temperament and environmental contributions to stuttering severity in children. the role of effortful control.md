# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Constructs:** Effortful Control (EC), Surgency/Extraversion, Negative Affectivity.
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-Short Form (CBQ). (p. 86)
- **Informant:** Mothers. (p. 86)
- **Response Scale:** 7-point scale (1 = extremely untrue to 7 = extremely true). (p. 86)
- **Reliability:** Internal consistency benchmark of 0.70 reached in 11/12 scales; sadness was 0.65. (p. 86)
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct:** Stuttering Severity.
- **Instrument/Category:** 8-point severity scale (0 = normally fluent to 7 = very severe). (Table 1)
- **Type:** Communication/Verbal expression difficulty.
- **Score Type:** Raw score (0-7); half numbers accepted. (p. 85)
- **Reliability:** Inter- and intra-judge reliability for clinician ratings was > 97%. (p. 87)
- **Informants:** Mothers (global daily average) and Clinicians (based on speech samples). (p. 85, 87)

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Home Environment:** Confusion, Hubbub and Order Scale (CHAOS) (15 items, 5-point scale; $\alpha = 0.79$). (p. 86)
- **Life Events:** Life Events Checklist (LEC) (Yes/No on events in last 12 months). (p. 86)
- **Standardized Tests:** EVT, PPVT, and TOLD (used for eligibility screening). (p. 85)

## 5.2 Substantive Conclusion
There is a significant negative association between Effortful Control (EC) and stuttering severity as rated by both mothers and clinicians. Specifically, children with lower EC scores tend to exhibit higher stuttering severity. This association remained significant even when controlling for other temperament dimensions (Surgency, Negative Affectivity) and environmental factors (CHAOS, LEC). Other temperament dimensions did not significantly predict severity in the regression models.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
- **Environment and Temperament:** Significant correlations were found between CHAOS scores and Negative Affectivity, as well as CHAOS scores and Effortful Control. (Table 4)
- **Environment and Severity:** CHAOS scores were significantly correlated with mother-reported severity ratings. (Table 4)
- **Rater Agreement:** Mother-reported severity and clinician-reported severity were significantly correlated ($r = 0.46, p < 0.01$), although mothers rated severity as significantly higher than clinicians did ($t(68) = 5.32, p < 0.001$). (p. 87)

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Use of validated instruments (CBQ, CHAOS).
- Strong inter-rater reliability for clinician outcomes.
- Rigorous use of hierarchical linear modeling to control for confounding variables.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Small sample size ($N=69$).
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inferences.
- Lack of information on participation rates and power analysis.
- No reported blinding of clinicians to the children's temperament profiles.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Manual review required.**

The study provides strong evidence for an association between the Rothbart dimension of Effortful Control and stuttering severity. However, the inclusion criteria regarding monolingual status (1D) cannot be confirmed from the text.
