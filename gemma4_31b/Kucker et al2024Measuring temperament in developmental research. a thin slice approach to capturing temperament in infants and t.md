# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
The study used two methods to assess three dimensions (Surgency, Effortful Control, Negative Affect):

- **Caregiver Report (CR):**
    - **Instrument:** Very short form of the Early Child Behavior Questionnaire (ECBQ) (p. 6).
    - **Informant:** Parent/Caregiver.
    - **Scale:** 7-point scale (1 = never, 7 = always).
    - **Reliability:** $\alpha$ Negative Affect = .67, Surgency = .75, Effortful Control = .71 (p. 6).
    - **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

- **Thin-Slice (TS) Ratings:**
    - **Instrument:** Modified ECBQ for naive raters (p. 7).
    - **Informant:** Naive undergraduate research assistants (p. 8).
    - **Items:** 18 items (6 per domain) (p. 7).
    - **Scale:** 7-point scale (p. 7).
    - **Reliability:** $\alpha$ Negative Affect = .96, Surgency = .82, Effortful Control = .93 (Table 1).
    - **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Instrument:** MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventory (MCDI) (p. 7).
- **Category:** Expressive vocabulary.
- **Measure Types:** 
    - MCDI: Words and Sentences (MCDI:WS) for children $\le 30$ months.
    - MCDI: III for children $> 30$ months.
    - Vocab percentile for the entire sample.
- **Score Type:** Raw count of words marked by caregiver (p. 7).

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
None reported.

## 3.4 Comparison Type
Continuous language ability (vocabulary size/percentile) compared with continuous temperament scores (composite means).

**Substantive Conclusion:**
The study found that thin-slice (TS) temperament ratings generally showed stronger associations with vocabulary than caregiver reports (CR), particularly in children 30 months and under. Specifically, TS Negative Affect was consistently negatively associated with vocabulary, while TS Surgency and Effortful Control were positively associated with vocabulary in the $\le 30$ month group. For toddlers $> 30$ months, only TS Negative Affect remained a significant predictor of vocabulary.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

The study reports convergent validity (correlations between TS and CR measures of the same trait) and divergent validity (correlations between different traits). All TS and CR dimensions were significantly correlated (r = .11 to .25) (Table 2).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
The study uses a large sample (N=516), employs a validated instrument (ECBQ), and provides rigorous reliability testing for the new thin-slice method, including interrater and intrarater reliability.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
The study is cross-sectional and lacks adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., SES, though reported descriptively). The sample lacks racial and ethnic diversity (predominantly White).

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include.**

The study provides empirical evidence of the association between temperament (both caregiver-reported and observer-rated) and expressive vocabulary in a developmentally appropriate age group. It specifically evaluates the validity of a new measurement approach (thin-slice) against an established Rothbart-based framework.
