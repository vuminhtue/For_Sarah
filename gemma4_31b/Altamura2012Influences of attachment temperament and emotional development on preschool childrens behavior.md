# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Measure:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-short version (CBQ-Short Form) (p. 37).
- **Informant:** Guardians.
- **Constructs:** 
    - **Negative Affectivity:** Average of Discomfort, Fear, Anger/Frustration, Sadness, and (reverse scored) Soothability.
    - **Extraversion/Surgency:** High Intensity Pleasure, Activity Level, Impulsivity, and (reverse scored) Shyness.
    - **Effortful Control:** Inhibitory Control, Attentional Focusing, Low Intensity Pleasure, and Perceptual Sensitivity.
- **Response Scale:** 7-point Likert scale (extremely untrue to extremely true).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Measure:** Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test - Third Edition (PPVT-III) (p. 38).
- **Construct:** Receptive vocabulary/screening test of verbal ability.
- **Score Type:** Raw scores converted to standard scores (Mean=100, SD=15).
- **Informant:** Child (administered task).

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Attachment:** Attachment Story Completion Task (ASCT) (p. 34).
- **Emotion Understanding:** Affect Knowledge Test (p. 39).
- **Emotion Regulation:** Emotion Regulation Checklist (ERC) (p. 39).
- **Behavioral Competencies:** Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) - Internalizing and Externalizing Scales (p. 40).

**Substantive Conclusion:**
There is a significant positive association between effortful control and receptive language ability (PPVT-III), whereas no significant associations were found between language and the Negative Affectivity or Extraversion/Surgency dimensions of temperament.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
- **Language and Behavior:** Higher language scores were significantly associated with lower externalizing behavior ($r = -.23, p < .05$) (Table 3).
- **Language and Attachment:** Higher language scores were associated with higher security ($r = .53, p < .01$) and lower deactivation ($r = -.47, p < .01$), hyperactivation ($r = -.41, p < .05$), and disorganization ($r = -.54, p < .01$) (Table 3).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
The study uses well-validated, standard instruments (CBQ, PPVT-III, CBCL) and employs rigorous statistical controls for key confounders (age, language, and SES/education).

### Main risks of bias and limitations
The study is cross-sectional, preventing causal inferences. The sample size is small (N=58). There is no reported power analysis, and information on the recruitment rate and potential selection bias is missing.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Manual review required.**

The article provides high-quality data extraction for temperament and language (including the core Rothbart dimensions and a standardized language test). However, the lack of explicit exclusion criteria regarding developmental disorders and explicit confirmation of monolingualism (beyond "English speaking") means the reviewer must verify if the sample aligns strictly with the "typically developing, monolingual" requirement.
