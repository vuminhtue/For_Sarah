# 2. Study and Data Extraction

## 2.1 Bibliographic information
- **Title:** Relation of motor, linguistic and temperament factors in epidemiologic subtypes of persistent and recovered stuttering: Initial findings
- **Authors:** Nicoline G. Ambrose, Ehud Yairi, Torrey M. Loucks, Carol Hubbard Seery, Rebecca Throneburg
- **Year:** 2015
- **Journal:** Journal of Fluency Disorders
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfludis.2015.05.004

## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
The manuscript describes a single multi-center longitudinal study. The analytic sample consists of children classified by their stuttering trajectory:
- **Persistent group:** Children who continued to stutter (n = 19).
- **Recovered group:** Children who showed complete remission (n = 39).
- **Control group:** Normally fluent children (n = 40).

## 2.3 Study design
- **Design:** Longitudinal cohort study with 7 visits.
- **Timing:** Data reported for Visit 1 (baseline, within 12 months of onset), Visit 3 (1 year later), and Visit 5 (2 years later).
- **Setting:** Four data collection sites in the Midwest US (University of Illinois, University of Iowa, Northern Illinois University, and University of Wisconsin at Milwaukee).

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled (CWS):** 81
- **Final analytic sample:** 98 (58 CWS + 40 NFC)
- **Exclusions:** 23 CWS were excluded because they did not complete the study long enough to be classified as Persistent or Recovered (Section 2.1.1).

## 2.6 Population characteristics and eligibility
- **Inclusion (CWS):** Within 12 months of onset; parental and clinician judgment of stuttering; severity $\ge 1$ on 0-7 scale; $\ge 3.00$ SLD per 100 syllables (Section 2.1.1).
- **Inclusion (NFC):** Negative history of stuttering; clinician judgment of no stuttering; severity $< 1$ on 0-7 scale (Section 2.1.2).
- **Exclusion:** Documented evidence of neurological disorders (Section 2.1.1).

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire Short Form Version I (CBQ) (Section 2.2.4).
- **Informant:** Parents (both mothers and fathers; results reported from mothers at Visit 1) (Section 2.2.4 / 3.4).
- **Dimensions:**
    - **Surgency/Extraversion:** Activity level, approachability, high intensity pleasure, impulsivity, and shyness.
    - **Negative Affectivity:** Anger/frustration, discomfort, fear, sadness, and soothability.
    - **Effortful Control:** Attentional focusing, inhibitory control, low intensity pleasure, perceptual sensitivity, smiling and laughter.
- **Response Scale:** 7-point Likert scale (1 = Extremely Untrue to 7 = Extremely True) (Section 2.2.4).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Vocabulary/General Proficiency:**
    - Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test-III (PPVT) - Receptive vocabulary (Section 2.2.3).
    - Expressive Vocabulary Test (EVT) - Expressive vocabulary (Section 2.2.3).
    - Test of Early Language Development (TELD) - Receptive (TELD-R) and Expressive (TELD-E) (Section 2.2.3).
- **Morphosyntax:** Mean Length of Utterance (MLU) and MLU-Z (Section 2.2.3).
- **Phonology:** Percent of Consonants Correct (PCC) for early, mid, and late developing phonemes (Section 2.2.3).

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Stuttering severity:** SLD (stuttering-like disfluencies), Repetition Units (RU), and 0-7 severity scales (Section 2.2.1).
- **Motor control:** Spatiotemporal Index (STI) for jaw displacement and Fundamental frequency (F0) variability (Section 2.2.2).

# 5. Temperament–Language Associations

This study does not report a direct correlation or regression between temperament and language variables. Instead, it identifies a **profile of the "Persistent" subtype**, where a specific temperament profile co-occurs with a specific language profile.

| Study | Temperament variable | Language variable | Analysis | Estimate type | Estimate | SE | 95% CI | p-value | Adjusted? | Covariates | N | Direction | Significant? | Location |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Negative Affectivity | Standardized Language (PPVT, EVT, TELD) | ANOVA / Group Comparison | Group Difference | NR | NR | NR | < .05 | No | NR | 98 | Persistent group: Higher Neg Affect / Lower Language | Yes | Sec 3.3.1 / 3.4 |

**Substantive Conclusion:** 
Children who persist in stuttering form a distinct subtype characterized by lower performance on standardized language tests (PPVT, EVT, TELD-R) and higher levels of Negative Affectivity (specifically higher fear and lower soothability) compared to children who recover from stuttering or typically fluent controls.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
- **Motor Variability:** The Persistent group also showed greater F0 variability at Visit 3, and the Recovered group showed greater kinematic variability (STI) at Visit 5 compared to controls (Section 3.2).
- **Phonology:** Both Persistent and Recovered groups showed lower accuracy for late-developing phonemes at Visit 1 compared to controls (Section 3.3.3).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Strong longitudinal design with multi-center data.
- Use of standardized, reliable instruments for both temperament and language.
- Clear distinction between persistent and recovered subtypes.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Loss to follow-up was higher than the 20% threshold (approx 28%).
- Lack of statistical control for potential confounders (e.g., SES, though reported as similar).
- Lack of blinding for assessors.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Manual review required.**

The article is scientifically highly relevant as it links the "Persistent" stuttering subtype to both higher Negative Affectivity and lower language ability. However, the lack of explicit reporting on monolingual status (Criterion 1D) requires a manual check of the full text or supplementary materials to ensure the sample meets the "monolingual" requirement.
