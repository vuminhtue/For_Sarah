# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Measure:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-Short Form (CBQ-SF). (p. 537)
- **Informant:** Caregiver. (p. 537)
- **Constructs:** Surgency (impulsivity, high-intensity pleasure, activity level, shyness), Negative Affectivity (fear, anger, sadness, discomfort, soothability), and Effortful Control (attentional focusing, inhibitory control, low-intensity pleasure, perceptual sensitivity). (p. 537)
- **Scale:** 7-point Likert scale ('extremely true' to 'extremely untrue'). (p. 537)
- **Reliability:** Reported as "satisfactory" (referencing Mullineaux et al. 2009). (p. 537)
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes. (p. 537)

## 3.2 Language
All measures were derived from a 15-minute conversational sample recorded during a clay-modeling activity (p. 535), transcribed and analyzed via SALT or CLAN (p. 536).

| Variable | Construct/Category | Score Type | Time Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| TCICU | Volubility (Total complete/intelligible C-units) | Raw count | HV2 & HV3 (Ave) |
| NTW-uncut | Word tokens (Total sample) | Raw count | HV2 & HV3 (Ave) |
| NTW | Word tokens (First 100 C-units) | Raw count | HV2 & HV3 (Ave) |
| MLU | Grammatical complexity (Morphemes/C-unit) | Ratio | HV2 & HV3 (Ave) |
| TNC-uncut | Grammatical complexity (Total conjunctions) | Raw count | HV2 & HV3 (Ave) |

## 5.2 Substantive Conclusion
The study found that temperament is significantly associated with language measures that reflect **volubility** (total count of words/units in a sample) but generally not with measures that control for volubility (e.g., MLU, NDW from first 100 units). Specifically, children higher in Surgency and Negative Affectivity tended to produce more language in total. The association between Surgency and TNC (standardized cut) was unexpectedly negative.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

- **Age:** Significant positive associations with NTW, MLU, TNC, TNC-uncut, and NDW (r = 0.24 to 0.30), consistent with developmental expectations. (p. 538)
- **Sex:** Boys scored significantly lower than girls on Perceptual Sensitivity (Effortful Control) and Sadness (Negative Affectivity). (p. 538)
- **Moderation:** Only one significant moderation effect was found: the correlation between Attention Focusing and Measure D differed by sex (Girls: -0.21; Boys: 0.25; z = -2.85, p < 0.01), though individual correlations were not significant at p < 0.01. (p. 540)

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Use of direct language samples rather than parent reports, reducing common source bias.
- Use of a recognized, valid temperament framework (Rothbart's CBQ-SF).
- High granularity in language measures, allowing for the differentiation of volubility from linguistic complexity.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Lack of longitudinal precedence (exposure not clearly before outcome).
- Reliance on parent reports for temperament, which can introduce bias.
- Lack of statistical adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., age, sex) in the primary association table.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include (Manual Review Recommended).**

The study is highly relevant as it directly addresses the confound of temperament/volubility in language sampling using a Rothbart-based framework. Manual review is required to determine if the inclusion of children who received speech-language services (22%) and the lack of explicit monolingual status reporting disqualify the sample based on the strict "typically developing, monolingual" criterion.
