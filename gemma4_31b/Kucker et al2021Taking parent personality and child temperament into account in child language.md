# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Instrument:** Early Child Behavior Questionnaire-short form (ECBQ).
- **Informant:** Parent.
- **Response Scale:** 1 (Never) to 7 (Always).
- **Dimensions & Scales:**
    - **Surgency:** Impulsivity, activity level/energy, high-intensity pleasure, sociability, positive anticipation.
    - **Effortful Control:** Inhibitory control, attentional shifting, low-intensity pleasure, cuddliness, attentional focusing.
    - **Negative Affect:** Discomfort, fear, motor activation, sadness, perceptual sensitivity, shyness, soothability, frustration.
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's alpha .67–.85 (dimensions) and .57–.83 (scales) for younger group; .70–.84 (dimensions) and .65–.83 (scales) for older group. (Note: Impulsivity was low: .31 and .34).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Vocabulary size:**
    - **Younger group (16-30m):** MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventory: Words and Sentences (MCDI-WS). Sum of words checked.
    - **Older group (31-42m):** MCDI-III. Sum of words checked.
- **Noun Vocabulary (Younger group):** Extracted from MCDI-WS sections 2-10.
- **Grammar/Complexity (Older group):** MCDI-III percentage of complex phrase and percentage of "yes" answers for complexity.
- **Mean Length of Utterance (MLU3):** Retrospective parent reporting of longest sentences; scored by research assistants. Inter-rater reliability > 90%.
- **Retrospective measures:** Age of word combining and number of new words in last 2 weeks.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Parent Personality:** Big Five Inventory (BFI). 44 items, 1-5 scale. Dimensions: Neuroticism, Extraversion, Openness, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness.

## 5.3 Substantive Conclusion
Across both age groups, child temperament (particularly Effortful Control) is positively associated with vocabulary size and other language markers (MLU, grammar). These associations are largely robust, remaining significant even after controlling for parent personality and child age. Effortful Control consistently emerged as the strongest and most robust predictor.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
The authors also tested **Parent Personality $\rightarrow$ Child Language**:
- In 16-30 month olds, parent conscientiousness and openness positively correlate with child vocabulary size (r = .18 and .18 respectively; Table 3).
- In 31-42 month olds, parent conscientiousness (r = .15) and agreeableness (r = .12) positively correlate with vocabulary size (Table 5).
- Hierarchical regressions showed parent personality explains unique variance in vocabulary size over and above child age (e.g., $\Delta R^2 = .04$ in younger group, Table 4).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Strong sample size for this type of research.
- Use of validated, widely accepted instruments (ECBQ, MCDI).
- Rigorous validity checks for MTurk data.
- Sophisticated statistical approach controlling for parent personality to address reporting bias.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- **Common Method Bias:** Both temperament and language are parent-reported, which may inflate correlations.
- **Design:** Cross-sectional design precludes causal inferences.
- **Selection Bias:** Reliance on MTurk (convenience sample) may limit generalizability.
- **Lack of SES data in models:** Authors acknowledge SES predicts language but did not include it in the regression models.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include.** 

The study provides high-quality empirical evidence on the relationship between core Rothbart temperament dimensions (Surgency, Effortful Control, Negative Affect) and language outcomes in early childhood, specifically addressing a key theoretical concern regarding parent-report bias.
