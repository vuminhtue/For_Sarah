## Overall inclusion determination  

**Include**  

The article meets all inclusion criteria. It investigates a Rothbart‑based temperament construct and standardized receptive/expressive vocabulary measures in a monolingual, typically developing preschool sample (3–5 yr) and reports explicit statistical associations between temperament and language.

## 2.2 Relevant Study Number  

- **Study 1** – the sole longitudinal cohort reported in this manuscript. It is the only study that examines temperament‑language links, therefore it is the focus of the extraction.

## 2.3 Study design  

- **Design:** Prospective longitudinal cohort with four assessment waves (≈7‑10 months apart) over 24‑32 months.  
- **Data source:** Participants drawn from a larger Vanderbilt longitudinal investigation of stuttering (Clark et al., 2015; Jones et al., 2014).  
- **Parent study citation:** Clark C. E., Conture E. G., Walden T. A., Lambert W. E. (2015).  
- **Recruitment:** Community advertisements, referrals from Vanderbilt Bill Wilkerson Center, and self‑referral (Methods 2.1).  
- **Location:** Vanderbilt University, Nashville, TN, USA.

### Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

*Justification:* The study clearly defines its population, uses validated instruments, and adjusts for major confounders, but it lacks a priori power analysis, does not report reliability for the temperament subscales, has substantial attrition (> 20 %), and the temporal ordering of exposure versus outcome is ambiguous. These issues lower confidence but do not render the evidence unusable.

#### Strengths  
- Longitudinal design with four repeated assessments.  
- Use of standardized, widely accepted language tests (PPVT, EVT).  
- Rothbart‑based temperament questionnaire with both full and short forms, allowing comparison across groups.  
- Inclusion of a well‑matched non‑stuttering control group.

#### Main risks of bias and limitations  
- **Attrition bias** (≈ 39 % loss).  
- **Measurement reliability** of CBQ subscales not reported.  
- **Temporal ambiguity**: temperament and vocabulary measured concurrently, limiting causal inference.  
- **Potential selection bias** due to voluntary participation and exclusion of bilingual children.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Protocol decision:** **Include** in the systematic review of child temperament and language.  

**Scientific relevance:** The article provides rare longitudinal evidence linking **negative emotionality** (a core Rothbart dimension) to **receptive vocabulary** in preschoolers with differing stuttering trajectories. It contributes both to the temperament‑language literature and to the specialized field of stuttering research.
