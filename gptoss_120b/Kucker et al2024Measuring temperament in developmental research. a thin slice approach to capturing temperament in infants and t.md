- One assessment wave (video + caregiver report) collected between 2015‑2020.  
- No experimental manipulation; associations examined via correlational and regression analyses.

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

*The study has clear aims, a well‑described Rothbart‑based temperament measurement, and adequate reliability. However, the lack of reported participation rate, unclear temporal ordering of exposure vs. outcome, absence of blinding information, and no adjustment for plausible confounders (e.g., child age, socioeconomic status) limit confidence in causal interpretation.*

### Strengths  

- Large, diverse sample of 516 infants/toddlers.  
- Use of a Rothbart‑based instrument (ECBQ) and a novel thin‑slice observational method with strong internal consistency and inter‑rater reliability.  
- Direct testing of temperament‑language associations using a validated expressive‑vocabulary measure.  

### Main risks of bias and limitations  

- Potential selection bias (only videos ≥ 13 min) with unknown denominator.  
- Temporal ambiguity (exposure and outcome measured concurrently).  
- No statistical control for age or SES, which are strong predictors of vocabulary.  
- Blinding of raters to language outcomes not documented.

---  

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Protocol decision:** **Include**  

The article meets all inclusion criteria, provides original empirical data on Rothbart‑based temperament and a qualified language outcome in a monolingual, typically developing sample within the target age range, and reports the required statistical associations.
