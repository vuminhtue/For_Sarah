## 2.3 Study design and data source  

* Design: Cross‑sectional parent‑report survey (online via Amazon Mechanical Turk).  
* Waves: Single‑time‑point assessment for each age group.  
* Data source: New data collected by the authors; not a secondary cohort.  
* Recruitment: U.S. adults meeting MTurk quality criteria (≥85 % approval, U.S. residence).  
* Setting: Remote/online.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation  

*Parent personality* was positively correlated with child vocabulary (e.g., Openness r = .18, Conscientiousness r = .18) and accounted for ~5 % of variance after controlling for age (Table 3, 4). These associations were used as covariates in the temperament‑language partial correlations.

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

*Rationale*: The study has a clear research question, well‑described population, adequate sample size, reliable measures, and appropriate statistical control for key confounders. Limitations include the cross‑sectional design (exposure not preceding outcome), lack of reported participation rate and attrition denominator, and absence of blinding, which collectively lower the quality to a fair level.

### Strengths  

* Large sample (N > 700 total) with power analysis.  
* Use of a validated Rothbart‑based temperament questionnaire.  
* Comprehensive language assessment (vocabulary, MLU, grammar, complexity).  
* Statistical control for parent personality, age, and gender.  

### Main risks of bias and limitations  

* Cross‑sectional, parent‑report data for both predictor and outcome (possible common‑method bias).  
* No information on recruitment pool → participation rate unknown.  
* Lack of blinding of outcome assessors.  
* Attrition details insufficient to assess loss‑to‑follow‑up.
