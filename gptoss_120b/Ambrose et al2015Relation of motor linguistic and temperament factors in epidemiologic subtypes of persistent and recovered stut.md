## Overall inclusion determination  

**Include**  

The manuscript meets all core inclusion criteria (age, temperament, language, empirical data, Rothbart measure, and reported associations). The only uncertainty concerns explicit monolingual status; however, this does not preclude inclusion because the sample appears to be typical U.S. preschool children without major neuro‑developmental disorders.

## 2.2 Relevant Study Number  

**Study 1** – the longitudinal cohort reported in the article; it is the only study within the manuscript that examines temperament‑language relations.

## 2.3 Study design and data source  

* Design: Prospective longitudinal cohort (observational).  
* Assessment waves used for the present analyses: Visits 1 (baseline, ≤ 12 mo post‑onset), 3 (≈ 1 yr later), and 5 (≈ 2 yr later).  
* Multi‑site data collection (University of Illinois, University of Iowa, Northern Illinois University, University of Wisconsin‑Milwaukee).  
* Participants recruited from day‑care centres, speech‑language clinics, advertisements, and self‑referrals.  
* The same children were followed across waves; the protocol was identical at all sites.

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

The study has a clear aim, well‑defined cohort, validated measures, and a longitudinal design, fulfilling many quality criteria. However, lack of reporting on participation rate, sample‑size justification, blinding, and especially the relatively high attrition (≈ 28 %) and absence of covariate adjustment lower confidence in causal inference, yielding a *Fair* rating.

### Strengths  
* Multi‑site longitudinal design with repeated assessments.  
* Use of established Rothbart‑based temperament questionnaire and standardized language batteries.  
* Direct comparison of persistent, recovered, and control groups.

### Main risks of bias and limitations  
* Attrition > 20 % may bias subgroup classification.  
* No adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., sex, SES, parental education).  
* Monolingual status not explicitly confirmed.  
* Reliability statistics for CBQ not reported within the article.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Protocol decision: Include** – the article satisfies the inclusion criteria for child temperament‑language research. The data contribute valuable longitudinal evidence on how negative affectivity relates to language outcomes in children who stutter, supporting the systematic review’s objectives.
