## Overall inclusion determination  

**Exclude**  

The manuscript does not assess a qualifying language ability or language disorder; the primary outcome is stuttering severity, which falls outside the language constructs defined for inclusion. Consequently the study does not meet the core inclusion criterion (1C).

## 2.2 Relevant Study Number  

Only **one** analytic sample is presented (69 children who stutter). It is the focus of the temperament‑severity analysis and thus the sole relevant study.  

## 2.3 Study Design and Data Source  

* **Design:** Cross‑sectional, observational (parent‑report questionnaire + clinician rating) within a larger longitudinal cohort of children who stutter.  
* **Assessment waves:** Single time‑point (all measures collected during the initial visit).  
* **Parent study:** Participants were part of a larger longitudinal study of stuttering subtypes (mentioned in “Children” section).  
* **Recruitment sites:** University of Illinois at Urbana‑Champaign; University of Iowa; University of Wisconsin; Northern Illinois University (mid‑west U.S.).

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

*Strengths*: Clear definition of sample, use of a validated Rothbart‑based temperament instrument, reliable severity ratings, and multivariate regression adjusting for relevant covariates.  

*Main risks of bias / limitations*: No reported participation rate or power analysis; cross‑sectional design prevents temporal inference; exposure and outcome measured simultaneously; outcome assessors not blinded; lack of information on language ability limits relevance to language‑focused reviews.  

---  

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Decision:** **Exclude**  

*Rationale:* Although the study meets most temperament‑related inclusion criteria, it does **not** assess a qualifying language ability or language disorder outcome (stuttering severity is a fluency disorder, not a language construct). Therefore the article does not satisfy the core inclusion criterion (1C) and is excluded from the language‑temperament synthesis.
