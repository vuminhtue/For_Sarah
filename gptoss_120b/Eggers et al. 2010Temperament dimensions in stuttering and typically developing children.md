## Overall inclusion determination  

**Exclude** – The article does not assess a qualifying language ability or language disorder; therefore it does not meet the core inclusion criterion requiring a temperament‑language association.

*Brief justification*: While the study provides robust data on temperament in children who stutter versus typically developing peers, it lacks any measure of language ability (vocabulary, expressive/receptive language, etc.) and consequently cannot contribute to a review of temperament‑language relations.

* Location: Dutch‑speaking part of Belgium (Lessius University College) and associated schools.

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair** – The study clearly defines its population and uses a reliable Rothbart‑based temperament instrument, but lacks information on participation rates, power, temporal ordering, blinding, and adjustment for confounders, limiting internal validity.

### Strengths  

* Use of a well‑validated, Rothbart‑based questionnaire (CBQ‑D).  
* Matched design (age ± 2 months, gender) between CWS and controls.  
* Comprehensive reporting of composite and scale‑level temperament differences.

### Main risks of bias and limitations  

* Cross‑sectional design prevents inference about causality or temporal precedence.  
* No language outcome measured, so the study cannot address temperament‑language relations.  
* Absence of reported participation rate and power analysis.  
* No adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., socioeconomic status).  
* Outcome assessors (parents) were aware of child’s stuttering status, raising possible reporting bias.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Exclude** – The article does not assess any qualifying language ability or language disorder; therefore it does not meet the inclusion criteria for a temperament‑language review.
