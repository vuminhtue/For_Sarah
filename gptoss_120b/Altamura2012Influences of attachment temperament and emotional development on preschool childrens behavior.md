## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

*Justification*: The study clearly defines its population, uses validated measures, and adjusts for important confounders, meeting many NIH criteria. However, lack of reported participation rate, absent sample‑size justification, cross‑sectional timing (exposure not preceding outcome), and no blinding of outcome assessors reduce overall methodological rigor.

### Strengths  

- Well‑validated, Rothbart‑based temperament questionnaire.  
- Standardized language assessment (PPVT‑III).  
- Comprehensive reporting of descriptive statistics and zero‑order/partial correlations.  
- Inclusion of relevant covariates (age, language, parental education).  

### Main risks of bias and limitations  

- Convenience sample with unknown recruitment yield → possible selection bias.  
- Cross‑sectional design limits causal inference; exposure and outcome measured simultaneously.  
- No power analysis; sample size modest (N = 58).  
- Lack of assessor blinding may introduce measurement bias for language testing.  

---  

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Protocol decision: Include** – the article meets all inclusion criteria and provides usable data on temperament‑language associations for the target age range. The study’s limitations (cross‑sectional, small N) are noted but do not preclude inclusion in a systematic synthesis.
