### Summary of substantive conclusions  

* **Surgency** shows the most consistent (though modest) positive correlations with language measures that reflect **volubility** (TCICU, NTW‑uncut, NDW‑uncut) and a negative correlation with TNC when derived from a standardized 100‑C‑unit sample.  
* **Negative Affectivity**, particularly the **Sadness** facet, also correlates positively with volubile language outputs.  
* **Effortful Control** shows no meaningful associations with any language metric.  
* Controlling for volubility (e.g., using MLU, NDW from 100 C‑units) attenuates the temperament‑language links, supporting the authors’ claim of discriminant validity for those measures.

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Overall rating: Fair**  

*Strengths*: clearly defined population, validated temperament instrument, reliable language transcription, appropriate statistical tests.  
*Main risks of bias*: unclear participation rate, no power analysis, ambiguous temporal ordering of exposure vs. outcome, lack of adjustment for potential confounders (age, sex), and no blinding of outcome assessors.

---  

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Protocol decision: Include** – the study satisfies all core inclusion criteria (age range, temperament, language outcomes, empirical English article, Rothbart‑based measure, reporting of core dimensions and statistical temperament‑language associations). The uncertainty regarding monolingual status does not outweigh the relevance of the data.

*Scientific relevance*: Provides rare school‑age data on how Rothbart‑derived temperament dimensions (especially Surgency) relate to naturalistic conversational language measures, directly addressing the review’s focus on volubility‑controlled language outcomes.
