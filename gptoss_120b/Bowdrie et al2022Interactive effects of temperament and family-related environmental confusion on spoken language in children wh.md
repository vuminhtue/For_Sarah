## Overall inclusion determination  

**Manual review required.**  
The study examines both an eligible TH subsample and a DHH subsample. The DHH group does not meet the “typically developing” criterion, creating mixed eligibility. A human reviewer should decide whether to include the TH portion only or exclude the article altogether.

## 5.3 Moderation plot (Figure 1)  

* The conditional effect of effortful control on language is **positive** at low (16th percentile) and moderate (50th percentile) CHAOS scores (b = 4.90, p < .001; b = 2.41, p < .001) and **non‑significant** at high CHAOS (84th percentile) (b = ‑0.82, p = .385). Johnson‑Neyman indicates the interaction becomes non‑significant when CHAOS > 29.15. (Figure 1, p. 1).  

## 5.4 Summary of substantive findings  

* In **DHH children**, higher **effortful control** is associated with better receptive language, but only when **family‑related environmental confusion** is low‑to‑moderate.  
* No significant temperament‑language links were found in the **TH** group.  
* Negative affectivity and surgency‑extraversion showed no direct association with language in either group.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation  

* CHAOS was positively correlated with effortful control in TH (r = 0.171) and negatively in DHH (r = ‑0.060), suggesting different family dynamics across groups (Table 3).  
* No other child outcome variables were reported.

## Overall NIH quality judgment  

**Fair** – The study has a clear objective, well‑validated measures, and appropriate statistical control for a major confounder, but limitations include lack of temporal ordering, no reported participation rate or power analysis, and no evidence of assessor blinding.

### Strengths  

* Use of Rothbart‑based, psychometrically sound temperament questionnaire.  
* Norm‑referenced language assessment appropriate for the age range.  
* Inclusion of both DHH and matched TH groups allows comparison.  
* Moderation analysis (PROCESS) with Johnson‑Neyman probing provides nuanced interpretation.  

### Main risks of bias and limitations  

* Cross‑sectional design precludes causal inference; exposure and outcome measured simultaneously.  
* Participation rate and sampling frame unclear; possible selection bias.  
* No power calculation; sample may be under‑powered for detecting smaller effects, especially in subgroup (HA vs. CI).  
* Outcome assessors not reported as blinded, raising risk of measurement bias.

# 8. Final Review Decision  

**Manual review required** – The manuscript contains both an eligible typically‑developing TH subsample and a DHH subsample that does not meet the “typically developing” criterion. A reviewer must decide whether to (a) include only the TH data, (b) exclude the entire article, or (c) treat the DHH group as a separate, relevant population despite the deviation from the protocol.
