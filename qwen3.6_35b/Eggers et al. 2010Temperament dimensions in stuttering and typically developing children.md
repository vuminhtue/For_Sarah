**Exclude.**

# 2. Study and Data Extraction

## 2.1 Bibliographic information
- **Title:** Temperament dimensions in stuttering and typically developing children
- **Authors:** Kurt Eggers, Luc F. De Nil, Bea R.H. Van den Bergh
- **Year:** 2010
- **Journal:** Journal of Fluency Disorders
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jfludis.2010.04.001

## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
- **Study Number:** Single study (part of a larger project described in Eggers et al., 2009).
- **Relevance:** Examines group differences in temperament between children who stutter (CWS) and typically developing children (TDC). Relevant for temperament measurement but excluded due to speech-focused outcome.

## 2.3 Study design and data source
- **Design:** Cross-sectional group comparison (CWS vs. TDC) with correlational analysis within the CWS group.
- **Waves:** Single time point assessment.
- **Data Source:** Part of a larger ongoing investigation; recruitment via speech-language pathologists (CWS) and school systems (TDC) in Dutch-speaking Belgium.
- **Setting:** Community/school-based recruitment in Flanders, Belgium.

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled:** 116 children (58 CWS, 58 TDC).
- **Final analytic sample:** 116 children.
- **Exclusions/Missing:** None reported for the primary analysis; matching criteria ensured equal group sizes. Factor analysis for structure validation used a larger preceding sample (N=215).

## 2.6 Measures

### Temperament Measures
- **Construct/Facets:** Extraversion/Surgency, Negative Affectivity, Effortful Control (3 composite factors); 18 subscales (e.g., Anger/Frustration, Inhibitory Control, Attentional Shifting).
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-Dutch (CBQ-D; Van den Bergh & Ackx, 2003).
- **Informant:** Caregivers (specifically mothers).
- **Items/Scale:** 233 total items; 7-point Likert scale ('extremely untrue' to 'extremely true').
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's α averaged .74 across 18 scales. | p. 360
- **Assessment time point:** Single baseline session.
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

### Language Measures
- **Construct:** NA (Study focuses on speech fluency/stuttering, not language).
- **Instrument/Task:** Stuttering Severity Instrument-3 (SSI-3) or Dutch equivalent (Test for Stutter Severity). Therapy duration (months).
- **Category:** Speech production disorder severity / treatment metric.
- **Score type:** Severity rating (mild to very severe); duration in months.
- **Reliability/Validity:** SSI-3 correlates highly (r = .94) with Dutch severity scale. | p. 359
- **Assessment time point:** Single baseline.

### Comparison Type
- Group comparison: Language-disorder/speech-disorder group (CWS) compared with typically developing group (TDC) on temperament scores.
- Correlation: Continuous temperament scores correlated with stuttering severity and therapy duration in CWS group.

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
Rothbart-based CBQ-D (parent-report). Composite factors: Extraversion/Surgency, Negative Affectivity, Effortful Control. Individual scales: 18 subscales covering reactivity and self-regulation dimensions.

## 3.2 Language
Not applicable. Study measures developmental stuttering severity (SSI-3) and therapy duration. No language ability, vocabulary, grammar, or language disorder metrics were collected or reported.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Stuttering severity:** SSI-3 or Dutch equivalent (categorical: mild to very severe).
- **Therapy duration:** Self-reported/clinical record months of fluency therapy (1–24 months).

---

# 4. Descriptive Statistics

Composite factor z-scores and individual CBQ scale means/SDs are reported in Tables 3 and 4. Group differences favored higher Negative Affectivity and lower Effortful Control in CWS. Individual scales showing significant group differences: Anger/Frustration, Approach, Motor Activation, Inhibitory Control, Attentional Shifting. Remaining 13 scales did not differ significantly.

---

# 5. Temperament–Language Associations

**None reported.** The manuscript explicitly compares temperament profiles between children who stutter and typically developing peers. No language ability, receptive/expressive language, or language disorder measures were administered or analyzed. Consequently, no temperament-language correlations or regression models are present.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

Only temperament-speech/stuttering associations were reported (see Table 2H above). All Spearman rank correlations between temperament factors/scales and stuttering severity or therapy duration in the CWS group were nonsignificant (p > .05). This suggests that in this sample, temperamental reactivity and self-regulation dimensions do not linearly predict reported stuttering severity or length of prior therapy.

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Fair**

### Strengths
Clear operationalization of temperament using a Rothbart-based, validated instrument. Well-defined, matched groups with transparent diagnostic criteria for stuttering severity. High internal consistency for temperament scales.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
Lack of covariate adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., SES, caregiver mental health, child cognitive ability). Cross-sectional design precludes temporal inference. No participation rate or power analysis reported. Blinding procedures not detailed.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Exclude.** 
The manuscript does not meet inclusion criterion 1C (language ability/disorder measure) and reports no temperament-language associations (criterion 1H). The study exclusively investigates temperament in relation to developmental stuttering (a speech production disorder), which falls outside the scope of language-focused screening criteria.
