# 2. Study and Data Extraction

## 2.1 Bibliographic information
- **Title:** Volubility as a mediator in the associations between conversational language measures and child temperament
- **Authors:** Laura Segebart DeThorne, Kirby Deater-Deckard, Jamie Mahurin-Smith, Mary-Kelsey Coletto, Stephen A. Petrill
- **Year:** 2011
- **Journal:** Developmental Science
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-6984.2011.00034.x

## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
- **Study/Cohort:** Single study embedded in the Western Reserve Reading Project (WRRP), a longitudinal population-based twin study.
- **Relevance:** Focuses directly on the relationship between caregiver-reported temperament and direct conversational language sample measures in school-age children.

## 2.3 Study design
- **Design:** Cross-sectional correlational analysis using data averaged across two annual home visits (HV2 and HV3).
- **Assessment waves:** Two home visits (HV2 and HV3); temperament questionnaire administered between visits.
- **Data source:** Western Reserve Reading Project (WRRP), a longitudinal cohort study.
- **Recruitment setting/location:** Metropolitan areas of Cleveland, Columbus, and Cincinnati, Ohio; primarily White, middle-class families.

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled sample:** 161 first-born twins (independent sample selected from twin pairs).
- **Final analytic sample:** Varies by measure due to minimum C-unit requirements: n = 124–161.
- **Subgroup sizes:** MZ twins (n=73), DZ twins (n=85), undetermined zygosity (n=3).
- **Exclusions/missing data:** Samples with fewer than 50 C-units were excluded for MLU; measures cut to first 100 C-units required n ≥ 100 available utterances per visit.

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Construct/Facet:** Surgency, Effortful Control, Negative Affectivity (plus individual facets: shyness, high-intensity pleasure, activity level, impulsivity, attentional focusing, inhibitory control, fear, anger, sadness, discomfort, soothability).
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-Short Form (CBQ-SF).
- **Informant:** Parent/caregiver questionnaire.
- **Items/Response scale:** 7-point Likert scale ("extremely true" to "extremely un true"); individual facets reverse-coded appropriately.
- **Reliability:** Alpha coefficients reported as satisfactory in prior analysis (Mullineaux et al., 2009).
- **Time point:** Administered between HV2 and HV3.
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes.

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct:** Conversational language use/volubility, lexical diversity, morphological complexity.
- **Instrument/Task:** 15-minute naturalistic language sample during clay-play interaction; transcribed per SALT conventions.
- **Measures:** TCICU, NTW (100-utterance cut & uncut), MLU (morphemes), TNC (100-utterance cut & uncut), NDW (100-utterance cut & uncut), Measure D.
- **Score type:** Raw counts, morpheme counts, ratio scores, and D values.
- **Reliability/Validity:** 90–93% transcription agreement for boundaries and morphemes; validity supported by developmental trajectories and correlation with standardized norms (cited).
- **Time point:** Averaged across HV2 and HV3.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Covariates/Controls:** Child age at HV2, biological sex (analyzed for moderation, not fully covaried in primary correlations).
- **Zygosity:** Monozygotic, dizygotic, undetermined (not controlled in correlational analyses).

**Substantive conclusion:** Measures that explicitly control for volubility (MLU, NTW-100, NDW-100) showed no significant associations with temperament. Frequency/volubility-dependent measures (TCICU, NTW-uncut, NDW-uncut) demonstrated modest but significant positive correlations with Surgency and Negative Affectivity/Sadness. Effortful Control showed no significant associations. Sex moderated only one association (Attention focusing vs. Measure D), with non-significant individual correlations.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation
- **Age associations:** NTW, MLU, TNC, TNC-uncut, and NDW correlated positively with child age at HV2 (r = 0.24–0.30, p < 0.01). Attention focusing correlated negatively with age (r = −0.25, p < 0.01), but this became nonsignificant (r = −0.19) after removing two bivariate outliers.
- **Sex differences:** Boys scored significantly lower on perceptual sensitivity (facet of Effortful Control) and sadness (facet of Negative Affectivity) than girls. No sex differences emerged for language measures.
- **Measure stability:** Language measures showed moderate stability across HV2 and HV3 (r = 0.47–0.59, p < 0.01).

### Overall NIH quality judgment
**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Clear research objectives and well-defined, validated measures for both temperament and language.
- Direct observational language sampling reduces common-source bias compared to parent-report-only designs.
- Rigorous transcription reliability and standardized protocols.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional correlational analysis limits causal/temporal inferences.
- Missing information on recruitment rates, blinding, attrition, and a priori power.
- Incomplete control for confounders (age/sex not uniformly covaried).
- Demographic limitations (predominantly White, upper-middle SES; monolingual status unreported) limit generalizability.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include**
The study directly addresses the prompt's focus on child temperament and language ability, uses a Rothbart-based framework, reports core dimensions, and provides explicit temperament-language correlations. While monolingual status and developmental eligibility details are unclear, the sample overlaps with the eligible age range and the data are highly relevant. Recommend inclusion with caution regarding population generalizability.
