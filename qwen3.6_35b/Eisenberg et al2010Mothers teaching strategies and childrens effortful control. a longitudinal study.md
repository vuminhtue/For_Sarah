## 2.2 Relevant Study Number
- **Study focus:** Single longitudinal study examining bidirectional relations between children's effortful control and maternal teaching strategies over three waves.

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Construct/Facets:** Effortful Control (EC) composite covering attention focusing, attention shifting, and inhibitory control.
- **Instruments/Tasks:** 
  - Parent/Caregiver Reports: ECBQ subscales (T1, T2) and CBQ subscales (T3).
  - Behavioral: Snack delay task (observed self-restraint).
  - Observer Reports: Adapted Infant Behavior Record (IBR) attention and persistence ratings.
- **Informants:** Mothers, nonparental caregivers, trained observers/research assistants.
- **Items/Scale:** 12–14 items per subscale (ECBQ/CBQ); 1–4 point scale for delay task; 1–5 point scale for IBR.
- **Reliability:** Cronbach's alpha ranges from .62 to .92 across reporters/tasks and time points; ICCs for coding .73–.99.
- **Time Points:** T1, T2, T3.
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes (ECBQ and CBQ).

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct:** Expressive vocabulary (word production).
- **Instrument:** MacArthur Communicative Development Inventories (CDI) Short Form Levels II (T1, T2) and IV (T3).
- **Score Type:** Total number of words spoken in English or Spanish.
- **Reliability/Validity:** Standardized checklist; validity established in CDI manuals; reliability not reported in this manuscript.
- **Time Points:** T1, T2, T3.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures (if any)
- **Maternal Teaching Strategies:** Observed directives, questioning, and cognitive assistance during a puzzle/Lego teaching task (coded on 1–3 scales).
- **Covariates:** Socioeconomic status (T1), child age, child sex, vocabulary (as covariate for EC models).

**Substantive Conclusion:** Effortful control positively and significantly correlates with expressive vocabulary across all three time points, particularly when reported by mothers. Structural equation models controlling for age, sex, and SES confirm that vocabulary positively predicts concurrent effortful control (T1 and T2). No significant associations were found between maternal teaching strategies and vocabulary. The temperament-language association is robust, positive, and bidirectional in direction within the longitudinal model.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

The study extensively reports associations between effortful control and *maternal teaching strategies* (directives, questioning, cognitive assistance), finding that higher EC predicts more cognitive assistance/questioning and fewer directives over time. These associations are statistically significant in zero-order correlations (Table 5) and longitudinal SEMs (Figs 1-3). SES was positively associated with T1 EC and maternal questioning, and negatively with directives. Age showed a weak negative correlation with cognitive assistance at T2.

### Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Good**

### Strengths
Strong longitudinal design with three waves, multimethod/multireporter temperament assessment, robust structural equation modeling controlling for autoregressive paths and key covariates, and high inter-rater reliability.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
Attrition of ~18% over 3 years; potential unmeasured confounding; lack of explicit blinding for behavioral coders; limited generalizability to lower SES or non-Western samples; vocabulary reliability not reported in-text.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Protocol decision: Include.**
The study provides high-quality longitudinal evidence linking a core Rothbart temperament dimension (effortful control) to expressive vocabulary in typically developing toddlers/preschoolers. The rigorous SEM approach controlling for stability and covariates strengthens causal inference regarding child-driven effects on maternal teaching, though the temperament-language link is primarily correlational/parallel in this design. Scientific relevance is high for developmental psychopathology and early language intervention literature.
