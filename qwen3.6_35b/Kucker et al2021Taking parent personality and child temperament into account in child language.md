## 2.2 Relevant study number and analytic samples
A single study examined two age-based subsamples: 
1. **Younger group:** Children 16–30 months old (N = 460).
2. **Older group:** Children 31–42 months old (N = 328).
Both subsamples report the core temperament-language associations relevant to the review.

## 2.3 Study design and data source
- **Design:** Cross-sectional correlational survey using parent report.
- **Waves/Timing:** Single assessment wave per child.
- **Data Source:** Amazon Mechanical Turk (MTurk); data archived on OSF.
- **Recruitment:** Online via MTurk; required >85% approval rating and US residency. Data collected Dec 2015–Apr 2019. (Methods, p. 5)

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled:** 788 parents (460 + 328)
- **Final analytic sample:** 460 (younger), 328 (older)
- **Exclusions:** 369 additional MTurkers excluded for validity check failures or diagnosed genetic disorders. (Methods, p. 5)

## 2.6 Measures

### Temperament Measures
- **Construct:** Surgency, Effortful Control, Negative Affect (broad dimensions + discrete scales)
- **Instrument:** Early Childhood Behavior Questionnaire-short form (ECBQ; Putnam et al., 2006)
- **Informant:** Parent/caregiver report
- **Items/Scale:** 1 (Never) to 7 (Always)
- **Reliability:** Cronbach’s α reported for dimensions (.67–.85 younger; .70–.84 older) and scales (.57–.83 younger; .65–.83 older)
- **Time point:** Cross-sectional (concurrent with language)
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes (explicitly noted)

### Language Measures
- **Construct:** Expressive vocabulary size, noun count, age of word combining, new words/2 weeks, MLU3 (younger); Vocabulary, grammar/complexity %, new words, MLU3 (older)
- **Instrument:** MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventory: Words & Sentences (MCDI-WS) and MCDI-III (Fenson et al., 1994, 2007)
- **Category:** Parent-report checklist/vocabulary survey; raw word counts and percentages
- **Reliability/Validity:** Established psychometrics in original manuals; internal consistency not re-calculated here as it is a checklist sum score
- **Time point:** Cross-sectional

### Comparison Type
Continuous language ability scores compared with continuous temperament dimension/scale scores via bivariate correlations, partial correlations, and hierarchical regressions.

**Substantive conclusion:** Child effortful control and surgency positively correlate with vocabulary, while negative affect negatively correlates. These bivariate associations are partially attenuated when controlling for parent personality, but effortful control remains robust. Temperament dimensions significantly predict additional variance in vocabulary beyond child age and gender in both age groups.

# 3. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

Parent personality traits (Big Five) were controlled for and reported alongside temperament-language associations. Parent conscientiousness and openness positively correlated with child vocabulary in both age groups. Hierarchical regressions confirmed that parent personality accounts for unique incremental variance in vocabulary above and beyond child age and gender (Table 4, Table 6). However, temperament effects persisted even after statistically partialing out parent personality, supporting independent temperament-language links.

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Large, well-powered sample with clear developmental age ranges.
- Use of validated, widely accepted measures (ECBQ, MCDI).
- Rigorous data screening and validity checks (MTurk protocols, inter-rater reliability for MLU).
- Appropriate statistical control for major confounders (age, gender, parent personality).

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal or directional inferences.
- Common-method variance due to sole reliance on parent report for both temperament and language.
- Convenience sampling via MTurk limits generalizability to diverse socioeconomic or geographic populations, though sample demographics are reported transparently.

# 5. Final Review Decision

**Protocol decision:** Include. 
**Scientific relevance notes:** The article directly addresses the prompt's focus by examining core Rothbart temperament dimensions in a typical development, monolingual sample within the eligible age range. It provides robust correlational evidence for temperament-language links while uniquely disentangling them from parent personality influences. The cross-sectional nature and parent-report design are noted limitations but do not preclude inclusion for evidence mapping.
