## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
- **Study Number:** Single longitudinal cohort study (7 visits over ~5 years; data from Visits 1, 3, and 5 reported).
- **Relevance:** Directly profiles temperament and language abilities across epidemiologic subtypes (Persistent vs. Recovered CWS vs. NFC) to examine persistence/recovery differentiation.

## 2.3 Study design
- **Design:** Multi-center longitudinal observational cohort.
- **Assessment waves:** 7 visits total; data analyzed from Visits 1 (baseline/onset), 3 (1 year later), and 5 (2 years later).
- **Data source:** Part of a larger multi-site longitudinal project on early childhood stuttering at four Midwest university sites.
- **Recruitment:** Daycare centers, SLP clinicians, health professionals, advertisements, self-referrals.

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled:** 81 children who stutter (CWS) + 40 normally fluent controls (NFC).
- **Final analytic sample:** 58 classified CWS (19 Persistent, 39 Recovered) + 40 NFC.
- **Exclusions/Dropouts:** 23 CWS did not complete the study or meet persistence/recovery criteria by Visit 5 (~38% attrition among CWS).

## 2.6 Measures

### Temperament Measures
- **Construct:** Surgency/Extraversion, Negative Affectivity, Effortful Control
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire Short Form Version I (CBQ)
- **Informant:** Parents (mothers and fathers; mother data primarily reported)
- **Items/Scale:** 94 items; 7-point Likert (1=Extremely Untrue to 7=Extremely True)
- **Reliability:** Not reported in this article; cited as well-normed with high validity (Rothbart et al., 2001; Putnam & Rothbart, 2006)
- **Time point:** Visit 1 (baseline)
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes

### Language Measures
- **Constructs:** Receptive vocabulary, expressive vocabulary, early language development (receptive & expressive), mean length of utterance, phonological accuracy (late-developing phonemes)
- **Instruments:** PPVT-III, EVT, TELD (TELD-R, TELD-E), MLU/MLU-Z, PEPPER system for PCC
- **Score types:** Standardized T-scores/norm-referenced (PPVT, EVT, TELD); raw/standardized (MLU); percent correct (PCC)
- **Reliability/Validity:** Established psychometric properties for PPVT-III, EVT, TELD; inter-rater reliability for transcription reported as 80.8% (narrow) and 92.3% (broad)
- **Time points:** Visits 1, 3, 5

### Comparison Type
Group comparison: Persistent CWS vs. Recovered CWS vs. NFC on standardized language scores and temperament subscales.

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
The study utilized the Children's Behavior Questionnaire Short Form Version I (CBQ), a Rothbart-based parent-report instrument measuring three core dimensions: Surgency/Extraversion, Negative Affectivity, and Effortful Control. Scores were derived on a 1–7 scale. The measure is well-established for assessing relatively stable dispositional traits in early childhood.

## 3.2 Language
Language was assessed using multiple standardized and observational tools: Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test-III (receptive vocabulary), Expressive Vocabulary Test (expressive vocabulary), Test of Early Language Development (receptive and expressive composite), Mean Length of Utterance (MLU), and Percent of Consonants Correct (PCC) from conversational samples focusing on late-developing phonemes. These measures cover receptive, expressive, vocabulary, and phonological domains.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures (if any)
Stuttering-like disfluencies (SLD), kinematic variability (STI), and fundamental frequency (F0) variability were measured but fall outside the core temperament-language focus. No additional covariates (e.g., nonverbal IQ, socioeconomic status) were statistically controlled in the primary ANOVAs.

# 5. Temperament–Language Associations

The study did not report direct correlational or regression analyses linking temperament scores to language scores within the same sample. Instead, it compared three groups (Persistent CWS, Recovered CWS, NFC) separately on temperament (CBQ) and language (standardized tests/MLU/PCC) at baseline. 

**Key findings:**
- Children classified as Persistent stutterers scored significantly higher on Fear and lower on Soothability (Negative Affectivity components) compared to Recovered and Control groups at baseline (p < .01).
- Persistent stutterers scored significantly lower than both Recovered and Control groups on PPVT, EVT, and TELD-R across multiple visits, indicating broader receptive and expressive language delays.
- No significant group differences emerged for TELD-E, MLU, or MLU-Z.
- Substantive conclusion: While direct temperament-language correlations were not tested, the parallel group differences suggest that children with persistent stuttering exhibit both elevated negative affectivity (greater fear, poorer soothability) and delayed standardized language proficiency compared to peers who recover or do not stutter.

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

Motor kinematic and acoustic measures were also compared across groups. Persistent stutterers showed greater jaw displacement variability at Visit 5 and greater F0 onset/offset variability at Visit 3 compared to controls. Phonological accuracy for late-developing phonemes was lower in both stuttering groups compared to controls at baseline. These findings position temperament and language deficits as concurrent early markers rather than isolated phenomena.

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Clear longitudinal cohort design with multi-site standardized protocols.
- Robust, validated measures for both temperament and language.
- Long follow-up period sufficient to observe developmental trajectories.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Attrition exceeded 20% among the stuttering cohort, potentially biasing group comparisons.
- Lack of statistical adjustment for potential confounders (e.g., SES, nonverbal cognition).
- No explicit blinding of outcome assessors to clinical group status.
- No direct statistical correlation between temperament and language reported; analysis relies on parallel group comparisons.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Include.** The article provides valuable longitudinal group comparison data profiling temperament (Negative Affectivity) and language (PPVT, EVT, TELD, PCC) in a preschool cohort with early-onset stuttering. Although direct temperament-language correlations are absent, the parallel domain profiling strongly supports subtype differentiation and aligns with the review's focus on stable temperament traits and language ability in young children. Manual review recommended for verifying monolingual status and extracting exact language test descriptives from Appendix B if needed.
