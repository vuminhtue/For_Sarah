## 2.6 Measures

### Temperament Measures
- **Construct/Facets:** Negative Affectivity, Extraversion/Surgency, Effortful Control
- **Instrument:** Children's Behavior Questionnaire-Short Form (CBQ-Short Form; Putnam & Rothbart, 2006)
- **Informant:** Parent/Guardian
- **Items/Subscale:** 36 items total; 3 broad aggregate factors calculated
- **Response Scale:** 7-point Likert (extremely untrue to extremely true)
- **Reliability:** Cites satisfactory internal consistency and criterion validity from Putnam & Rothbart (2006); alpha/omega not reported for this specific sample.
- **Assessment Time Point:** Single cross-sectional administration
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes

### Language Measures
- **Construct:** Receptive vocabulary / Verbal ability screening
- **Instrument:** Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test - Third Edition (PPVT-III)
- **Category:** Receptive vocabulary
- **Score Type:** Standard scores (Mean=100, SD=15); raw scores converted
- **Reliability/Validity:** Cites Dunn & Dunn (1997) for internal consistency and criterion validity
- **Assessment Time Point:** Single cross-sectional administration
- **Comparison Type:** Continuous language ability compared with continuous temperament scores (zero-order and partial correlations)

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
CBQ-Short Form parent report assessing three broad Rothbart dimensions: Negative Affectivity, Extraversion/Surgency, and Effortful Control.

## 3.2 Language
PPVT-III child-administered receptive vocabulary test providing standard scores used as a continuous language development measure.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures (if any)
- Attachment Story Completion Task (ASCT; Q-sort coding for Security, Deactivation, Hyperactivation, Disorganization)
- Affect Knowledge Test (Emotion understanding)
- Emotion Regulation Checklist (ERC; Emotion Regulation & Lability/Negativity subscales)
- Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL; Internalizing & Externalizing behavior)

---

# 4. Descriptive Statistics

Table 2 (p. 43) provides complete descriptive statistics for all primary temperament and language variables (N=58 each). Means and SDs fall within expected normative ranges for the respective instruments. Language scores (M=105.61, SD=12.17) align closely with the PPVT-III normative mean of 100. Temperament factor scores range between ~3 and ~6.5 on the 1-7 Likert scale, with Effortful Control showing the highest mean (5.39) and Negative Affectivity the lowest (4.24).

---

# 5. Temperament–Language Associations

| Temperament Variable | Language Variable | r | p | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Effortful Control | PPVT-III | 0.42 | < .01 | Significant positive association |
| Negative Affectivity | PPVT-III | -0.07 | NS | Non-significant association |
| Extraversion/Surgency | PPVT-III | 0.04 | NS | Non-significant association |

**Substantive Conclusion:** Only Effortful Control demonstrated a moderate, statistically significant positive correlation with receptive vocabulary development. Negative Affectivity and Extraversion/Surgency showed negligible, nonsignificant associations with language scores. All temperament-language correlations are zero-order; partial correlations controlling for age, language, and guardian education are not directly applicable for re-testing temperament-language links (language is the control variable), but the author notes effortful control was significantly higher as language scores increased (p. 44).

---

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

- **Demographic Controls:** Guardian education significantly correlated with lower disorganization/hyperactivation (r=-.24*, r=-.26*), lower ERC lability/negativity (r=-.26*), and lower CBCL internalizing (r=-.23*) and externalizing (r=-.25*) scores (Table 3, p. 46).
- **Behavioral Outcomes:** Effortful Control significantly negatively correlated with both Internalizing (r=-.394**) and Externalizing behavior (r=-.394**). Negative Affectivity positively correlated with Internalizing (r=.375**) and Externalizing (r=.403**) (Table 5, p. 54).

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Clear research objectives and well-defined measures using validated instruments (CBQ, PPVT-III, CBCL).
- Explicit reporting of continuous variables and appropriate statistical controls for demographic confounders.
- Transparent reporting of correlations and regression models in structured tables.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference or temporal precedence.
- No reported power analysis or participation rate limits generalizability and power assessment.
- Heavy reliance on parent-report for temperament and behavior introduces shared-method variance risk.
- Lack of blinding and single-wave assessment restricts causal and developmental interpretation.

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Protocol decision:** Include.
**Scientific relevance notes:** The manuscript directly addresses the prompt's focus by measuring Rothbart-based temperament dimensions and receptive vocabulary in a preschool sample, reporting their association, and controlling for demographic factors. Although limited by cross-sectional design and parent-report bias, it provides valuable zero-order correlation data on how self-regulatory temperament relates to early language development.
