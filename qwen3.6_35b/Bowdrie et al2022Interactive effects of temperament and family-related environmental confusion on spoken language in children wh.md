## Overall inclusion determination

**Include.** The manuscript directly examines the association between Rothbart-based temperament dimensions and receptive language ability in early childhood. # 2. Study and Data Extraction

## 2.1 Bibliographic information
- **Title:** Interactive Effects of Temperament and Family-Related Environmental Confusion on Spoken Language in Children Who Are Deaf and Hard of Hearing
- **Authors:** Kristina Bowdrie, Rachael Frush Holt, and Derek M. Houston
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research
- **DOI:** 10.1044/2022_JSLHR-21-00665

## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
This manuscript reports a single cross-sectional correlational and moderation study drawing participants from a larger longitudinal study on developmental outcomes in children who are DHH. The analysis focuses on the concurrent association between caregiver-reported temperament, family-related environmental confusion, and child receptive language.

## 2.3 Study design
Cross-sectional correlational design with moderation analysis. Data were collected via home visits where caregivers completed questionnaires and trained researchers administered child language testing.

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled:** 117 families (TH = 59; DHH = 58)
- **Final analytic sample:** Varies slightly by measure due to missing data (e.g., 1 DHH child unable to complete CELF Preschool-2; DHH language analysis N = 57).
- **Subgroups:** TH (typically hearing) and DHH (mild-to-profound sensorineural hearing loss). Within DHH: HA users (n = 26) and CI users (n = 32).

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Construct:** Effortful control, negative affectivity, surgency-extraversion (core Rothbart dimensions)
- **Instrument:** Children’s Behavior Questionnaire–Short Form (CBQ-SF; Putnam & Rothbart, 2006)
- **Informant:** Primary caregiver (all mothers or fathers/grandmothers)
- **Items/Subscales:** 94 items loading onto 3 dimensions
- **Response scale:** 7-point Likert (1 = extremely untrue to 7 = extremely true)
- **Reliability:** Subscale α = .62–.88 (12 of 15 subscales > .70)
- **Time point:** Single wave (caregiver report of behaviors over the past 6 months)
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct:** Receptive spoken language
- **Instrument:** CELF Preschool-2 (Concepts & Following Directions subscale) for <6 years; CELF-5 (Following Directions subscale) for ≥6 years
- **Category:** Receptive language (standard/scaled scores)
- **Score type:** Standardized/scaled scores
- **Reliability:** Norm-referenced, standardized administration; reliability/validity details per test manuals not specified in manuscript
- **Time point:** Single wave (during home visit)

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- **Construct:** Family-related environmental confusion
- **Instrument:** Confusion, Hubbub, and Order Scale (CHAOS; Matheny et al., 1995)
- **Informant:** Primary caregiver
- **Items:** 15 items
- **Response scale:** 4-point Likert (1 = very much like our home to 4 = not at all like our home)
- **Reliability:** α = .79
- **Time point:** Single wave
- **Covariate:** Caregiver’s highest level of education (10-point ordinal scale)

**Substantive Conclusion:** In children who are deaf and hard of hearing, effortful control positively predicts receptive language, but this association is significantly moderated by family-related environmental confusion. The positive link holds only when environmental confusion is low-to-moderate (CHAOS ≤ 29.15). At high levels of confusion, effortful control no longer predicts language. No significant temperament-language associations were found in the typically hearing group.

# 6. Additional Associations Relevant to Interpretation

- **Temperament–Environment Associations (DHH):** Effortful control negatively correlated with CHAOS (r = -.270, p < .05). Negative affectivity positively correlated with CHAOS (r = .472, p < .05). Surgency-extraversion not significantly correlated with CHAOS (r = -.102).
- **Temperament–Environment Associations (TH):** Effortful control negatively correlated with CHAOS (r = -.277, p < .05). Negative affectivity positively correlated with CHAOS (r = .337, p < .05). Surgency-extraversion positively correlated with CHAOS (r = .279, p < .05).
- **Negative Affectivity Main Effect:** In the DHH group, negative affectivity significantly predicted poorer receptive language in its regression model (β = -5.07, p = .041), though the overall model was non-significant (p = .133).
- **Location:** Table 3, Table 4, Results section.

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Clear research objectives and well-defined population criteria.
- Use of validated, widely accepted temperament and language instruments.
- Appropriate statistical controls (caregiver education) and examination of moderation effects.
- Transparent reporting of descriptive statistics, correlations, and regression outputs.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference regarding temperament and language.
- No power analysis or sample-size justification provided.
- Single-informant (caregiver) reports for both temperament and environmental confusion introduce potential shared-method variance.
- Lack of reporter blinding for outcomes.
- DHH subgroup has a major sensory/developmental condition, limiting direct generalization to typically developing monolingual populations despite the TH control group.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Protocol decision:** Include.  
The article reports a direct statistical association between Rothbart-based temperament dimensions (effortful control, negative affectivity, surgency-extraversion) and child receptive language ability. It meets age, measure, and empirical availability criteria. The significant moderation by environmental confusion in the DHH group provides substantive temperament-language interaction data. Manual review may be warranted if the focus is strictly on typically developing monolingual samples only.
