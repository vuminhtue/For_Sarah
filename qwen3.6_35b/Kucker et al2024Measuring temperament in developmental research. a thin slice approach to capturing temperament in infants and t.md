## 2.2 Study number and analytic samples
- **Study/Analysis:** Single archival re-analysis (labeled as the present study/Study 1 in preregistration).
- **Relevance:** Directly tests criterion validity of a novel thin-slice temperament method by correlating temperament scores with concurrent caregiver-reported vocabulary size.

## 2.3 Study design and data source
- **Design:** Cross-sectional archival video re-analysis.
- **Assessment Waves:** Single time point for the current analyses (temperament and language measured originally at lab visits between 2015–2020; re-coded here concurrently).
- **Data Source:** Archived videos from the first author's lab (2015–2020). Data linked to OSF: https://osf.io/jex53/
- **Recruitment:** Central, southern, and midwestern United States; recruited from a range of socioeconomic backgrounds.

## 2.4 Sample size
- **Total enrolled/available:** 516 videos met criteria (≥13 min length, consented for future research).
- **Final analytic sample:** Varies by analysis. Full sample N = 516 for reliability/validity checks. Correlation analyses used N ≈ 355–496 depending on the specific MCDI form or percentile calculation.
- **Exclusions:** 22 participants missing caregiver temperament; 3 missing vocabulary data dropped from respective analyses.
- **Location:** Participants section, pp. 5–6; Table 4 footnote, p. 8.

# 3. Measures

## 3.1 Temperament
- **Constructs:** Negative Affect, Surgency, Effortful Control.
- **Instruments:** 
  1. *Caregiver-report:* Very short form of the Early Childhood Behavior Questionnaire (ECBQ; Putnam & Rothbart, 2006). 7-point scale (1=never, 7=always). Cronbach's α = .67 (NA), .75 (Surgency), .71 (EC).
  2. *Observer-report:* Modified thin-slice coding form adapted from ECBQ. 18 items (6 per domain). 7-point scale. Internal consistency ranged from adequate to good (α = .82–.96).
- **Informant:** Caregivers (ECBQ) and naive undergraduate raters (thin-slice).
- **Timepoint:** Concurrent with original lab visits (2015–2020).
- **Rothbart-based:** Yes. Explicitly maps to Rothbart's three broad dimensions.
- **Location:** Measures section, pp. 5–6; Table 1, p. 7.

## 3.2 Language
- **Construct:** Expressive vocabulary size and vocabulary percentile.
- **Instrument:** MacArthur-Bates Communicative Development Inventory (MCDI: Words & Sentences for ≤30 mos; MCDI: III for >30 mos).
- **Format:** Caregiver checklist; raw word counts and standardized percentiles.
- **Reliability/Validity:** Well-established normed instrument for young children's expressive vocabulary.
- **Timepoint:** Concurrent with original lab visits.
- **Location:** Measures section, p. 6; Table 4, p. 8.

## 3.3 Other relevant measures
- None reported. The study focused strictly on temperament and vocabulary.

**Substantive Conclusion:** Both thin-slice and caregiver-report temperament dimensions show significant, expected directional associations with vocabulary in the younger age group (≤30 months). Effortful control consistently shows the strongest positive correlations (~.26–.30). Negative affect shows significant negative correlations in thin-slice ratings but not caregiver reports for this age group. Associations weaken or become nonsignificant in the older toddler group (>30 months), likely due to reduced sample size and MCDI:III psychometric properties.

## Overall NIH quality judgment

**Overall rating: Fair**

### Strengths
- Clear research objectives and preregistration.
- Large, well-characterized sample with robust psychometric reporting for temperament measures.
- Use of a validated, standardized language measure (MCDI).
- Transparent reporting of reliability, convergent/divergent/criterion validity, and both significant and nonsignificant associations.

### Main risks of bias and limitations
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference or temporal ordering.
- No statistical adjustment for key confounders (e.g., child age, SES, caregiver education) in the reported temperament-language correlations.
- Limited racial/ethnic diversity (86.1% White, primarily English-speaking), limiting generalizability to broader populations.
- Archival data constraints mean temperament and language were measured under varying original lab conditions not fully standardized for temperament capture.

---

# 8. Final Review Decision

**Protocol decision: Include**

The study directly addresses child temperament and language ability in a typically developing, monolingual sample within the target age range. It uses a Rothbart-based framework, measures stable temperament traits, and reports direct statistical associations with expressive vocabulary. The article meets all core inclusion criteria and provides valuable criterion-validity data for a novel temperament measurement approach.
